# Chapter 7: Prometheus Array

## 1

A massive mechanical arm reached out and seized the decelerating ship, locking it onto the docking bridge's rail. The view outside the window was replaced by steel walls, with dim yellow lights flashing past one after another.

The ship began sliding down along the descent track.

Listening to the rhythmic clunking from beneath the hull, Phoenix could tell they were moving steadily through the docking bridge — like riding an elevator. The gravity indicator on the wall ticked slowly upward as the ship drew closer to the surface.

She counted the seconds in her head. The lights in the bridge grew brighter, the descent gradually slowing, until she could make out the details of the equipment mounted on the iron walls.

*Clunk — CLANG!*

A sharp impact rang out. Phoenix suddenly realized she was no longer in a vacuum — the sound felt startlingly loud.

She looked out the window. The ship had stopped descending. She guessed they had reached the platform, though the angle of the window made it impossible to see.

The cabin door slid open on its own. Hime reappeared, apparently done with her duties.

"This way, please. The gravity here might feel a little off at first, but don't worry too much — just remember to go easy with your hands."

Disar walked carefully, taking in the view along the ship's corridor. Phoenix, by contrast, seemed completely at ease. They rounded a few corners and reached the ship's main exit — and the sight that greeted them stopped them both cold.

Steel frameworks interlocked in every direction, radiating a cold, futuristic chill. Enormous mechanical arms hung above the port, efficiently unloading cargo from several docked vessels, their constant mechanical roar filling the air. Sharp alert tones sounded at intervals, each one marking another ship returning from space to the surface, while tremors underfoot announced the ignition of departing ones.

Small utility vehicles of every kind bustled across the metal floor — transporting, loading, working. Personnel in work uniforms moved between them, consulting and cross-checking.

Holographic displays cycled through the latest updates. Floodlights blazed across the entire port, bright as midday. Without the enormous clock hanging overhead, there would be no way to tell day from night.

The whole port was like a technological leviathan, and the sound of its machinery was its heartbeat.

"Good lord." Disar stared, then turned to Phoenix. "Hey — Phoenix, you okay?"

She looked at her with a puzzled expression.

Phoenix's face held no surprise. Instead, something harder to name had settled over her features — a blend of relief, excitement, and a quiet sadness.

"I'm fine, Disar. It's just… seeing this port, I felt something I can't quite explain. Moved, I guess. Just… moved."

Phoenix swept her gaze across the port and murmured softly, "This is something."

A figure in the distance grew clearer — a staff member hurrying toward them with a tablet, who snapped a salute to Hime.

"Warrior Hime, good work out there."

"Just doing my job." Hime smiled. "Any new orders?"

"Marshal Fujin just issued a directive — the two witnesses are to be taken directly to the interrogation wing. Intelligence debrief as soon as possible."

"Understood. Is flight permitted in the port today?"

"Of course. Go right ahead."

Hime looked down at Phoenix and Disar. "Have either of you flown before? Do you get motion sick?"

The two exchanged a glance, then shook their heads.

"Then let's not waste time — hold on tight!"

Hime pulled both girls close with one arm each. A mechanical hum rose from her feet, and in an instant they were airborne.

"Here we go!"

She adjusted her posture mid-air, and with a roar from her leg thrusters, shot forward.

## 2

"AAAAAHHHHH—"

"AAAAAHHH — THIS IS AMAZING—!!"

The two of them screamed in turns as they streaked through the air, drawing a cluster of robots to swivel their cameras upward.

Even at a moderate speed, Phoenix and Disar were loving every second of this unexpected flight.

"Want me to do a flip?"

Hime asked.

"YES!" "NO!"

"Too late anyway — we're here!"

Phoenix looked ahead. A massive iron blast door stood wide open before them, its frame nearly half a meter thick.

"This is the port's main entrance. No flying past this point." Hime slowed gradually, touched down on her toes, and took a few quick steps to absorb the landing, setting both girls down smoothly. "The interrogation wing is just nearby."

"Interrogation wing?" Phoenix blinked.

Disar seemed to put it together immediately and waved her hands in alarm. "W-wait, interrogating *us*? We haven't done anything wrong — we're not going to jail, are we?!"

Hime laughed. "No jail, no punishment. You're witnesses to a new enemy encounter. We just need to gather some intelligence — about what you saw, and about you."

Phoenix and Disar both exhaled.

"Hold on — I need to take a call."

Hime turned away and pressed a finger near her ear. Phoenix and Disar exchanged uncertain glances, catching the quiet "copy" as she responded.

A moment later she turned back, smiling at them both.

"Miss Phoenix, Miss Disar — Marshal Fujin has just issued new orders. There's been a change of plan. You'll each be questioned separately. Please follow me."

Separately?

Phoenix fell into step behind Hime without a word, crossing an open stretch of floor and turning into a corridor.

"Um… Miss Hime…"

Disar asked quietly.

"Yes?"

"Why separately? We were together the whole time."

"I'm sure the Marshals have their reasons." Hime kept walking, not turning back. "Even a slight difference in angle can produce very different memories. Information gathered independently and then cross-referenced is far more reliable."

Disar looked down, then glanced sideways at Phoenix.

"You going to be okay?" she asked.

"What?" Phoenix said.

"I mean — I'm worried about you being alone again."

"It's not like a Roc is going to show up in there!"

## 3

Not scared? That would be a lie.

Phoenix admitted it to herself.

What had started as an ordinary interstellar trip had somehow escalated into a defensive battle, and now here she was, about to be questioned at the largest military base in the galaxy — all of it crammed into twelve hours, and happening to two teenagers, no less.

Her boots struck the iron staircase, the sound echoing through the vast space.

She looked down. The Prometheus Array's military port was nothing like the airports back on Ceres Major. Beyond the main gate wasn't a road or an open plaza — it was a sprawling base that stretched as far as she could see. Blue-grey metal formed the bones of this steel fortress, and harsh white floodlights stood in for sunlight.

"Room 1 is for Miss Phoenix — all the way to the end of the corridor. Miss Disar, you're in Room 13."

Hime placed her palm against a panel beside the sealed iron door. A device activated immediately, projecting a pale blue screen.

"Identity confirmed." A flat mechanical voice. The door split horizontally down the middle and retracted — top half rising, bottom half sinking.

Hime turned to face them. "Alliance regulations — I'm not permitted to accompany you inside. I'll wait at the door."

Phoenix nodded and stepped into the corridor. A sharp, unfamiliar smell hit her immediately — something she was certain she had never encountered before.

The corridor seemed to stretch deeper than it should. The amber lighting felt like a warning, like the glow of a place she wasn't supposed to enter. She thought she could hear her own bones creaking, like gears that hadn't been oiled in years. A low unease crept in, stirring up emotions she couldn't name, tangling them together. It was as if someone had grabbed the hem of her jacket and was whispering in her ear: *don't go in*.

Caught in that tide of feeling, Phoenix stopped walking.

But then she heard something else — a familiar set of footsteps. Disar's footsteps. Light and quick, the way they always were, because Disar always pushed off from the ball of her foot, her soles tapping out a clean, crisp rhythm on the floor.

Disar is right behind me, Phoenix thought. No reason to be afraid.

She walked on, her boots landing with a dull thud, blending with Disar's lighter steps into a simple, uneven percussion.

Right. Her best friend was at her back.

Then the footsteps behind her stopped.

Phoenix turned. Disar was standing in front of a silver iron door marked with a large red "13." She smiled and waved, mouthing: *I'm going in first!*

Phoenix watched her friend's figure disappear behind the door.

For a moment the corridor held only her. But she already knew someone had always been there.

She turned and walked to the end of the hall. The door marked "1" looked older than the rest. She raised her hand and knocked lightly.

"Excuse me?" she said softly.

"Come in." A young man's voice from inside.

Phoenix pushed the door open. The white light reflecting off the metal table hit her eyes. When she adjusted, she found herself looking directly at the person across from her.

A streak of orange ran through silver-grey hair. Yellow-tinted goggles made it take Phoenix a few seconds to identify the actual color of his eyes — dark grey. At the center of his black tailcoat, a faint blue point of light pulsed softly — the unmistakable mark of a Shipsian.

"H-hello…"

"No need to be nervous. Please, sit." The man flipped through a few sheets of documents. "Yigothu was supposed to handle this, but that guy pulled a disappearing act and filed for leave. Lucky him. Let's make this quick — I've got a vacation to get to myself."

Phoenix's smile came out slightly stiff.

"Right then. I'm Nimbus, Major, Galactic Alliance Army. I'll be asking you a series of questions. Please answer honestly, and hold any questions of your own until the end. Understood?"

"Understood."

"First question: your name is Phoenix — correct?"

## 4

"Thank you for your time!"

Disar bowed to the interviewer and stepped out into the corridor. She pulled the door shut behind her and looked toward Room 1.

"Not out yet?" She glanced at Phoenix's door, then drifted slowly toward it.

Might as well wait.

The soundproofing in here wasn't great. Standing close to Room 1, Disar could catch fragments of what was being said inside.

"Can you confirm that the Shipsian known as Shogun had his communications temporarily severed after being struck by an unknown weapon?"

"Confirmed."

Disar relaxed slightly. She'd been asked that question too — second to last. One more question, and Phoenix would be done.

If she remembered right, the next one would be—

"Can you confirm the presence of an enemy craft that appeared to possess stun capabilities?"

Right. That was the last one.

"Confirmed."

Disar exhaled. So Phoenix's debrief was almost—

"Have you ever experienced unusual dreams? For example — strange mechanical sounds, or voices?"

Hm?

Disar went still.

What was that? A new question? Why hadn't she been asked that?

"Yes."

"Could you describe the dream in detail?"

"I often dream about a beeping sound nearby, and a group of people saying things like 'module nominal' and that sort of thing."

"Miss Phoenix — in your daily life, do you get injured often?"

"No. My family always said I was tough — no matter how hard I knocked into something, I never really got hurt."

"Last question, Miss Phoenix — have you ever experienced memory loss?"

Disar heard Phoenix hesitate.

"Yes. I can't remember anything before I started school. My mom said I hit my head in a car accident, and we moved to a new place afterward. But since everything around me was new anyway, I never really felt like anything was missing."

"That concludes the debrief. You're free to go, Miss Phoenix."

"Thank you."

The iron door swung open. Phoenix poked her head out and found Disar already there.

"Disar — you waited for me?"

"Of course."

"Ready to go?"

"Let's."

"I'm kind of hungry."

"There has to be a cafeteria somewhere, right?"

"Let's ask Miss Hime."

"You think they'll feed us?"

"Is it too much to assume they will?"

"We won the battle."

"Did *we* really win it?"

"We were at least the best assist."

"Open the door, open the door."

"Easy on the handle!"

The two of them chatted and laughed their way to the corridor's main door. Phoenix took hold of one side — with the lightest possible grip. On a low-gravity planet, every piece of equipment had to be treated with care.

Under both their watchful eyes, the iron door swung slowly open.

The person standing on the other side was not Hime.

A deep brown coat. Short, neat sandy hair.

"Two little heroes. It's very good to meet you."

Phoenix recognized him almost instantly.

"Proxymar… Commander?"

## 5

*Bang. Clack-clack.*

Neni's brow was locked in a hard line. She bit down, fired a shot, and worked the bolt.

*Bang. Clack-clack.*

The target popped up. Within a tenth of a second, another round punched through dead center.

"Venting?"

Shogun had appeared at her side at some point, and asked.

"Yeah."

Neni answered flatly, hands never stopping as she swapped out the magazine.

"You look like a woman with her mind made up." Shogun said. "You might as well have written your next sentence on your face."

"Shogun, I…"

"You want to join them." He continued. "You hate those people. Don't you."

"What do they think we *are*?!" Neni suddenly snapped, her voice cracking into something close to a shout. "They knew all along that Outpost was going to be torn apart by those Void bastards! What did they think Outpost's death meant?! What did they think Banshee's suffering was worth?!"

She threw down the rifle and kept shouting at the target rising in front of her: "The Void Army killed Outpost. They ripped the weapons off her body — arms and all — and turned them against us. And those people *knew*. They knew it was coming, and they told us to sit there and take it!!"

Shogun caught the burning look in Neni's eyes.

"This change is coming, sooner or later." He closed his eyes. "If you've truly made up your mind to join it — if you're ready to give everything for this fight — then these old bones of mine won't be the ones to stop you. But don't act out of anger."

"— Shogun. I've decided. I'm done watching from the sidelines. This isn't anger talking."

Shogun let out a long breath and looked up at the ceiling.

The sound of a bullet finding its mark rang out again.
