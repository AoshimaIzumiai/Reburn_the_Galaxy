# Chapter 6: After the Storm

## 1

"…wake…"

"Phoe…"

She heard something.

The sound was muffled, like someone had wrapped her ears in layers of paper.

"…nix…"

Someone seemed to be calling her name.

She tried to respond, but found she couldn't make a sound. Her vision was a blur — she could just barely make out a figure moving in front of her. She guessed it was Disar.

With every ounce of strength she had, she curled a finger.

"She moved…"

"Phoenix! She's awake!"

The voices grew clearer.

What happened? she thought.

As her hearing came back, she blinked hard, and her vision slowly sharpened.

She realized she was lying in a bed. The surroundings were unfamiliar. Disar stood at her bedside, watching her with barely concealed anxiety. Above her head, a massive mechanical arm hovered in place.

"Heartbeat stable. Blood pressure normal."

The arm spoke through a small speaker. Its long cylindrical camera lens caught the light — it looked like a single unblinking eye.

"This is…"

Phoenix tried to speak. The moment she got out the first syllable, something felt off — her lips had dried and stuck together. What came out was a garbled, barely recognizable sound.

"This is the ship's medical unit — don't worry!"

Disar seemed to understand anyway, and answered quickly.

The words helped Phoenix focus. Medical unit? She blinked, staring up at the mechanical arm.

The arm produced a damp cloth from somewhere and pressed it gently to Phoenix's lips.

"This unit is the standard AI medical system aboard Alliance Patrol Vessel 210-A, designation MEDIC09."

The medical arm announced this with what seemed like pride, gesturing toward the ceiling.

"Initiating physical assessment. Please cooperate with the examination. Evaluating auditory damage. Please shake your head."

Phoenix obediently gave her head a firm shake. The arm chimed softly.

"Proceeding to verbal response check. Please state your name."

The arm removed the damp cloth. Phoenix moved her lips experimentally — she could open her mouth now.

"Phoenix."

Her voice came out rough and hoarse.

"Please state your home planet."

"Ceres Major."

"Please state the name of a friend."

"Disar. D-I-S-A-R."

"Brain scan complete. No physical damage detected. Initiating visual assessment. Please identify the colors displayed on screen."

A small screen extended from beneath the arm. Phoenix found it oddly fascinating.

"Please state the color shown."

The screen flashed red.

## 2

The moment that red glow bloomed before her eyes, everything came rushing back.

Lasers. Bullets. An endless rain of debris drifting through the stars — theirs and the enemy's.

Explosions bursting in silence. The red flicker of cannon muzzles. The crimson alarm lights blazing overhead.

Sirens, detonations, the roar of gunfire — it all crashed into her mind at once. Her expression froze. Pure terror flooded her eyes.

Red light.

The floor seemed to tilt. A sharp pain stabbed behind her eyes, and a wave of formless dread began rising from somewhere deep inside her.

Clang. Clang. Clang. Sounds that didn't exist echoed through the cabin — the cabin.

Phoenix suddenly realized she wasn't sitting in a bed at all. She was back in that cramped control chamber, and the streaks of light outside the glass were the burning wreckage of enemy ships.

She couldn't breathe. She thrashed her head wildly, but whatever was strangling her throat was still there — what was it? A strap?

A seatbelt. The operator's seat harness — one strap, two straps, three — binding her to the chair. She tried to struggle, but couldn't move. She tried to scream, but no sound came out.

Red light.

In an instant, a pinprick of red outside the window began to grow. The air felt like it had caught fire — her throat, her lungs, her whole body burning with it.

Doomsday Laser. White text appeared before her eyes out of nowhere. Not just once — the same two words kept materializing, over and over, filling every corner of her vision except for that red. Doomsday Laser, Doomsday Laser, Doomsday Laser…

The red light swallowed everything: wreckage, shells, stars. The white letters were stained blood-red, twisting and warping, until each one became a single screaming word: *DEATH*.

And then it was right in front of her face.

"AAAAAH!!"

Phoenix screamed in pure terror, arms and legs flailing wildly.

"I don't want to die! I don't want to die!!!"

"Phoenix! Calm down! Phoenix!!"

Disar watched her friend's expression shatter in an instant — and then Phoenix was thrashing on the bed like she'd lost her mind. Disar froze in a panic, not knowing what to do, calling her name over and over.

## 3

"Emergency detected. Assessment suspended. Sedative protocol engaged." The medical AI shut off the display immediately. The arm moved fast, pressing something that looked like a small patch onto Phoenix's arm.

"Phoenix, wake up! Help her — please, help her!"

Disar watched Phoenix still writhing in agony and shouted desperately at the medical unit.

The machine was silent for two seconds.

"Protocol approved. Authorized for use."

A brief mechanical whir — and then an antenna extended from the unit.

A high-pitched tone filled the room. It was sharp enough to make Disar's teeth ache. She instinctively clapped her hands over her ears, but it didn't help — the sound went straight through.

"Miss Disar, please follow this unit's instructions." The medical arm spoke calmly. "Grip the patient firmly at the back of the neck."

Disar gritted her teeth against the drilling sound, raised her hand, pushed Phoenix's blue hair aside, and pressed her fingers hard into the back of her neck.

The next few seconds stretched like hours.

One, two, three…

…eight, nine, ten.

"You may release now."

At the arm's prompt, Disar realized Phoenix had gone still — almost instantly.

She let go, half-convinced it hadn't worked, and said quietly: "Phoenix?"

"Disar!"

Phoenix sat bolt upright, startling a yelp out of Disar.

"Disar, what just—"

"Alliance Army Military Emergency Sedation Signal. This treatment immediately regulates a brain in acute distress, such as a panic state."

"So it's like… a sedative?" Disar asked.

The medical arm didn't answer directly. Instead, it said with an air of mystery: "Specific details are classified military information. This unit requests that both misses keep this confidential."

Phoenix raised her hand and studied her fingertips. Then her gaze traveled slowly up her arm to her shoulder.

"Nothing happened?" She looked at Disar in confusion. "The Doomsday Laser… the Roc…?"

"Look!" Disar spread her arms wide. "We made it out!"

"How — how is that possible?"

Phoenix grabbed Disar's arms, voice rising.

"It was a shield, Phoenix! Right before I blacked out, I saw a pale purple shield expand and wrap around both of us. When I woke up, we were on this ship."

"This ship… it said patrol vessel, right?"

Both of them turned to look at the medical unit.

"Correct. This vessel is a Galactic Alliance Army ship, currently returning to base." The arm answered in its flat electronic tone.

Before either of them could say anything more, the cabin door chimed.

"Scan complete. ID confirmed: G.A.A.S. Hime. Captain-level clearance verified. Door unlocked."

"May I come in?"

A gentle voice came from outside. Three unhurried knocks — warm, composed, and somehow immediately reassuring.

*Captain?* The voice was oddly familiar to Phoenix. It made her think, for no reason she could name, of the lilies that grew in the flower beds back home.

"Um… come in!" Disar called.

The door slid open.

## 4

A young woman stepped inside — early twenties, with silver-grey hair and an air about her like warm sunlight. Her rose-pink eyes were soft and full of warmth, and gold and yellow accents ran through her dress. Most striking of all was the small crown sitting quietly atop her head.

Phoenix also noticed the ornament at her waist. She recognized the design immediately — a Chrono Field emblem. That meant the captain standing before them was a Shipsian.

The woman smiled and walked toward them with unhurried, graceful steps.

"How are you feeling? Any lingering discomfort?" she asked gently.

Disar shook her head, then glanced over at Phoenix.

"Just now…" Phoenix wasn't sure how to describe what had happened — the way she'd completely lost control of herself.

"Phoenix experienced a brief acute stress response, but there is no lasting damage. Additionally, Phoenix has sustained some strain in her arms, which has been treated. A few days of rest will be sufficient for full recovery."

The medical arm answered for her before she could find the words. Phoenix found herself oddly grateful that the AI could read the room.

"No lasting damage — that's a relief." The Shipsian smiled. She reached behind her and produced something, holding it out to both of them. "Alliance Army random-flavor candy. Kids love these. I happened to have a few on me — want to try?"

*Random-flavor candy?* Phoenix took one and exchanged a glance with Disar.

*Don't take candy from strangers.* That was genuinely the first thing that crossed her mind.

"Um — Captain… miss, are you a Shipsian?" Disar asked first.

"Of course." The woman smiled. "I'm not as strong as most warriors, and my position comes with certain limitations, so I mostly handle support and patrol duties."

Phoenix thought for a moment, then asked her own question.

"What's your name, miss?"

"My name is Hime."

Both of them went still for a beat — then spoke at the exact same time:

"The royal princess of Zhey Auna?!"

Hime looked mildly startled, then a little shy. "It's really just a small kingdom… but yes, that's right. Though I much prefer being a warrior — it's a lot more interesting than sitting in a palace all day, don't you think?"

*Most people would kill to sit in a palace all day,* Disar thought privately.

"Aren't you going to try the candy?" Hime nodded toward the pieces still in their hands.

Well — she'd introduced herself. No reason to be suspicious now. Phoenix popped hers in first.

"Ugh — it's so bitter—" Disar grimaced, tongue out, looking deeply betrayed.

"Dovacoon bitter melon flavor." Hime laughed softly. "Apparently the locals who stay on as technicians there absolutely love pickled bitter melon. Safe to say the rest of the galaxy hasn't acquired the taste."

Phoenix turned the flavor over slowly on her tongue. It was sweet — not cloying, but a long, quiet sweetness, with something almost floral underneath.

"It's gently sweet," she said.

"Is it?" Hime looked at her with quiet attention. "That's Blight Chrysanthemum. A flower that used to grow on Blight — it went extinct in the war. A lot of warriors are fond of that flavor."

*Blight Chrysanthemum.* Phoenix remembered it — a flower she'd only ever seen in books. Grey-yellow petals, a faint blue at the center—

— the only living thing in the entire galaxy that had been erased completely, among the ruins of a broken world.

## 5

Hime took both their hands in hers and looked at Disar warmly. "You showed remarkable courage — you helped the Galactic Alliance Army win a battle today. That's no small thing."

Then she turned to Phoenix. "Attacking a Roc on your own initiative — most human soldiers in the Alliance wouldn't dare. It's a good thing Aries gave you that medal. Without it, this would have been a very different story."

"The medal?" Phoenix blinked.

Then it hit her. She looked down at her chest — the pocket was empty. She patted herself down frantically, but the medal was gone.

Hime let out a soft, bell-like laugh. She reached into the pocket of her dress and produced it — the very medal Aries had given Phoenix.

"This medal isn't ordinary. Aries modified it." Hime explained. "He installed a small personal shield trigger inside."

*A personal shield.*

Phoenix's eyes went wide. That was Zen — a Shipsian's Zen ability.

"It was only enough for one Shipsian warrior, but you two were close enough together that it covered both of you. It's a one-time trigger, though — a medal this small can only store so much energy." Hime held it out, and Phoenix closed her fingers tightly around it.

"It activates through a pressure sensor. I believe it was triggered when Disar held you close. Phoenix — you should thank your friend."

Phoenix turned to Disar — but before she could say a word, Disar covered her mouth with a flustered hand.

"Ahem, I just — I did it without thinking. It's nothing. Really. Don't."

Phoenix remembered what Aries had said.

*"If you truly insist on returning it, then do so at a Galactic Alliance Army base."*

"Miss Hime," Phoenix said, gently moving Disar's hand aside, "could you return this medal to Aries for me? It belongs to him — it should go back to him."

"I think you can return it yourself." Hime straightened up with a smile. "Because we're almost there. Open the window — let them see."

As if the cabin understood her, a low mechanical hum began, and one of the walls slowly descended — revealing a panel of glass in its place.

Beyond the glass was a stretch of space blazing with light. Stars were moving — no, not stars. Those were the searchlights of spacecraft. Hundreds of ships in motion, their lights blinking and sweeping like a meteor shower that never ended.

A spaceport stretched across the horizon, vast enough to wrap around an entire planet, bathing the surface below in a sea of gold. Phoenix watched a massive docking bridge slowly pivot and extend toward their ship.

"0046-210-A confirmed. Class-A and Class-B protected personnel detected aboard. Orbital descent approved. Prepare for landing."

"This doesn't look like Zhey Auna," Disar said. "We still have our Shipsian blood test there…"

"Don't worry — the relevant staff here can administer the test as well." Hime smiled. "I'll go confirm the docking now — excuse me for a moment. Buckle up. And—"

"—welcome to the Prometheus Array. Headquarters of the Galactic Alliance Army."
