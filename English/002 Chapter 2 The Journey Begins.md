# Chapter 2: The Journey Begins

> "The War of Blight was an atheist's offering to the gods."
>   — Saber, former Marshal of the Alliance Army, from a public address.

## 1
As the ceremony drew to a close, the crowd began to thin. At first, a few enthusiastic spectators lingered, speaking loudly about the earlier events, or seizing the chance — now that the square wasn't so packed — to snap a few photos together.
Phoenix had come alone, and she stayed behind too — though not for any photos. Within ten minutes, the plaza had emptied of every last Ceres Major resident. Every last one, except for her.

"Miss, why haven't you left yet?"

A gentle male voice came from behind. Phoenix turned to look — it was the gentleman in the blue suit who had bumped into her in the crowd earlier. Standing beside him was another man with silver-grey hair and dark sunglasses, apparently his companion.

"Hm? Can I help you, sir?"

Phoenix turned to face Aries, who towered over her by quite a bit.

"I bumped into you earlier… I'm truly sorry about that."

Aries bowed again, leaving Phoenix a little flustered.

"Please, sir, there's really no need!"

Phoenix waved her hands and bowed back in a bit of a panic.
The two of them stayed locked in that awkward standoff until the companion stepped in, straightened Aries up by the shoulder, and signaled Phoenix to stand as well. Then he draped an arm around Aries and said: "Don't mind him, miss — this guy's a gentleman to the bone. Polite as can be in everyday life, which is fine, but he takes that same careful, measured pace onto the battlefield too, and let me tell you, it drives me absolutely crazy. Right, Aries?"

Aries responded to his partner's teasing with good-natured resignation. "My apologies. This is my partner, Starless. I am Aries."

"Phoenix." She gave them both a salute — the standard Galactic Alliance Army salute, which also happened to be her favorite way to say hello.

A smile tugged at the corner of Aries's mouth. He studied her form for a moment, then brought his hands together in a quiet clap. "That's a textbook salute, Miss Phoenix. Better than quite a few Alliance Army recruits, I'd say."

Phoenix was puzzled. Recruits? Could he be in the Galactic Alliance Army?

"Are you a member of the Galactic Alliance Army?"

"I am."

A note of excitement crept into Phoenix's voice.

"Are you Shipsian?"

Aries smiled. "Yes."

Phoenix nearly leapt on the spot. Joining the Galactic Alliance Army had been her dream since childhood — and even more than that, she had spent every waking moment hoping that the mysterious Shipsian bloodline would one day awaken in her. She had held every Shipsian in deep admiration for as long as she could remember. Right now, she felt less like herself and more like a devoted fan who had just come face to face with her idol.

"I've wanted to join the Alliance Army my whole life!"

The words burst out of her before she could stop them.

"I want to be Shipsian — I want to be a warrior like you!"

Starless and Aries both paused, then exchanged a glance. Starless was the first to laugh, clapping his partner on the shoulder; his slightly raspy voice gave his laughter a distinctive sound. Aries smiled with quiet composure, turning his gaze back to Phoenix.

"Then keep at it, Miss Phoenix."

Aries looked as though he was about to say something more — but his watch chimed.

"We must be going. If you don't mind, please accept this."

Aries gave a slight bow and reached into the breast pocket of his suit, producing something that looked like a medal. He held it out to Phoenix. She took it with both hands, a little hesitant, and looked closer — it was a Galactic Alliance Army insignia.

"This was my insignia when I was first a recruit. The back has an emergency beacon — if your life is ever in danger, press the button three times in a row and it will call the nearest Galactic Alliance Army unit for support."

"Something this valuable…"

Phoenix startled and quickly tried to hand it back.
Aries only shook his head. "I trust you."

Starless glanced at Aries, then added: "I'm sure you understand what it's for, Miss Phoenix. Please don't send a false signal when you're perfectly safe. This thing is meant for use in wartime."

"But I can't keep something so important to you — how would I even return it?"

"If you truly insist on returning it, then do so at a Galactic Alliance Army base."

## 2
Ceres Major, Residential District XVIII.

"So you just… said goodbye and left?"

A girl with short black hair and blue eyes stared at Phoenix in mild disbelief.

"Hehe — pretty cool, right Disar? Isn't this badge amazing? Jealous? Your childhood best friend is just that lucky! Hehe."

Phoenix was showing off to the girl named Disar.
But Disar's face showed no trace of excitement. Instead, she delivered one supremely dry remark.

"…You didn't even ask for a photo?"

The air went still.

"Huh?"

Phoenix froze for a moment — then let out a wail.

"Aaaaaaaaaah!"

"Honestly, you…"

Disar pressed a hand to her forehead with a long-suffering sigh.

"If only Disar had come with me! Then I wouldn't have missed the photo!"

"There's a big exam right after we get back from Zhey Auna. I can't just skip studying… Besides, the ceremony will be on replay. Television exists."

Disar rolled up a few sheets of math problems into a makeshift baton and gave Phoenix two light taps on the head with it.
Phoenix shot her lifelong best friend a deeply aggrieved look.
Phoenix and Disar had known each other for as long as either of them could remember. The same dreams, nearly identical grades — they had grown up inseparable. Classmates, neighbors, best friends, childhood companions — none of those words quite captured the bond between them.

"Quiz question: name the Shipsian warriors who fell in the War of Blight."

"Striker, Flare and Phantom. Easy — you're not going to catch me off guard with that."

Phoenix answered with full confidence.

"You still dropped four spots in math last time." Disar tapped her on the head with the roll of papers again.

"Disar is the best at everything… learns fast, learns well, and her physical stats are off the charts… that's Shipsian all the way, isn't it!" Phoenix grabbed the rolled-up test. "All the teachers say Disar is going to be someone great."

Disar shook her head with a sigh, said "you're not half bad yourself," and put the test away.

"Tomorrow, we set off for Zhey Auna. Disar — this is the day we've been waiting for."

Phoenix murmured it quietly, gazing out the window.
Disar leaned against the bedframe, looking up at the clear blue sky.

"Let's make our dreams worth waiting for. To the Galactic Alliance Army — together, Phoenix."

"Together, Disar."

## 3
"Knock knock."

A wooden door at the corner of the street was rapped upon.

"Unbelievable — I fought hard for this vacation… Why does everyone keep bothering me?"

An impatient voice came from inside.

"…It's me. Trinity."

Trinity lowered his voice and knocked again.

"…Who?"

The person inside drew closer, asking again through the closed door, their tone edged with disbelief.

"Trinity."

"Impossible. That guy is still rotting away in his filthy little den with a bottle of something, same as he has been for ten years. He doesn't go out. He definitely doesn't come looking for me."

"Yigothu, I need a favor."

"Don't bother me, I've got experiments to run."  The footsteps inside moved away.

"Yigothu?"

No answer.

"Yigothu."

Trinity called out again.
He let out a sigh, then spoke once more: "I'm here to ask for help. If you won't open the door… I'll resort to force."

Still no response.

"I hope there's nothing flammable in there."

Bang!

Trinity vanished from outside the door in an instant. A sharp whoosh — and he reappeared inside.

"Hey!"

Yigothu shot up from his chair. His right hand snapped into a fist, and a low, droning hum filled the air as a pale white field expanded outward, sealing the entire wooden shack.

"Put away that Darkfire Blast! Are you trying to ignite my whole lab?!"

Yigothu pointed at Trinity and bellowed in his unsettlingly raspy voice.

Trinity shook his head and removed his hat. His clenched fists slowly opened. The black powder that had been spreading — almost imperceptibly slowly — through the air around him was drawn back into a faintly blue device on his left forearm.

Once the Darkfire Blast was fully reabsorbed, Yigothu lowered his arm. His right hand relaxed, and the field vanished in the blink of an eye.

“You nearly gave me a heart attack. That lab cost me six months’ salary. Put a leash on your Teleport!”

"You have a Chrono Field of your own." Trinity made himself at home, picking up a chair and sitting down. "How have you been?"

"Work, work, and more work. Lucky for me they gave me two weeks off for taking out an enemy squad. This damned war never ends — kill, kill, kill, when does the killing stop?! I used to be a rear-line researcher, you know that — now I've somehow become a first-rate soldier!"

"Got any drinks?" Trinity asked.

"Only that 'Shipsian Nutritional Supplement' stuff — want some?" Yigothu tossed over a bottle of pitch-black liquid with clear distaste. Trinity unscrewed the cap, gave it a sniff, then silently screwed the cap back on.

"After all these years, whatever they're making keeps getting worse." Trinity dropped the bottle into the trash can beside him.

"Agreed." Yigothu continued: "You've gone far out of your way to come here. What do you want? You should be grateful, you distinguished Ana-Shipsian — your wanted poster isn't plastered on any wall in this alley."

"I'm a thousand times better than anyone chasing me." Trinity's fingers drummed on the armrest of the chair. "The rebels who got taken down before — they were just weak. My Aura and Zen are more than enough to handle that lot."

Yigothu let out a short laugh — though his unsettling voice made it sound more like a cold sneer.

"Is that so? I'd almost like to see what a hero looks like after losing his Main Weapon."

"I'm here today because I need you to fix this."

Trinity tapped his chest. A point of blue light blinked to life — but it flickered erratically, stuttering between dim and bright.

"Can you fix it." Trinity coughed twice.

Yigothu's expression turned serious. "Fix that?"

"Correct."

"You want to go back to the battlefield?"

"I just need to know if you can manage it."

"…That's not beyond me."

"Then fix it."

"Why?"

"What do you mean, why?"

"The only reason you'd fix that is to fight. You gave up fighting for humanity… so why come back?"

"The reason is simple."

Trinity's eyes lit with something they had never held before — a flame of hatred. He said nothing, only looked at Yigothu.

Yigothu seemed to understand in an instant. His brow furrowed deep, and then he swore under his breath.

"…Those bastards."
