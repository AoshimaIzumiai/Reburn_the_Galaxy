# Interlude I: Blight Chrysanthemum Candy

"You gave them candy?"

Shogun sat nearby, both legs locked into some kind of complex mechanical brace. He watched Hime step inside and check — twice — that the door was locked behind her. He read the complicated look on her face, and shook his head like a man who already knew the answer.

"— Blight Chrysanthemum flavor." Hime murmured. "It's her. The intel was right."

"Didn't think you'd pull a move like that, Your Highness." Neni waved a hand and fished out two random-flavor candies of her own, popping one into her mouth. "There it is again. That sweetness."

Hime sighed and looked at the two of them.

"No need to tease. Everything Yigothu told us checks out." She shook her head and continued.

"The girl named Disar said the medal came from Aries. How did you explain it to them?" Shogun turned the medal over in his fingers.

"I told them Aries had installed a small personal shield trigger in the back — his own modification, designed to activate in a crisis."

Neni snorted at that, like she found the explanation a little funny.

"If new recruits got that kind of treatment, Cervidae wouldn't have been blown down to half a ship on its first deployment last year." She looked at the wall. "They're just kids, after all."

"If I remember right — when Aries was on Ceres Major, Starless was with him?" Shogun tossed the medal to Hime. She caught it with a small, unhurried motion.

"That's right. Both of them."

"The situation is a lot more complicated than we thought." Shogun said quietly.

"Are you saying Aries and Starless are on *his* side?" Neni spread her hands.

"I don't think so." Hime walked to the table and set the medal down. "They felt like they were going with the flow — not acting on that person's orders, and not like they were protecting the plan either. Aries is watching. Waiting. Or maybe — hedging his bets."

"I assumed they were on the side of keeping things as they are," Neni said. "Starless, at least."

"Those two are partners. Their thinking runs together." Shogun shook his head. "But if even Aries is sitting on the fence… which of us Shipsians is actually still backing this plan?"

"Things just keep getting more interesting." Neni picked up the medal.

"Yigothu has been working for my old friend all along. Beyond that, no one else's position is confirmed." Shogun exhaled.

"Setting that aside for now — how are you two holding up?"

Hime looked between them.

"I bounced back fast enough." Neni rolled her ankle experimentally. "Lucky that stun turret never learned how to cause explosive damage — it just knocked my systems offline for a while. Otherwise I'd be joining that plan myself."

Shogun looked down at his legs. "I took it worse than Neni. I only just got comms back online. Main Weapon and Zen are functional, but my Aura is still stuttering — and my leg thrusters are completely out. That's the downside of these old bones." He paused. "Might as well use this as an excuse to ask for an upgrade."

The three of them laughed quietly together.

"Back to business." Hime unclipped a device from her wrist. It projected a holographic display. "We're heading back to Zhey Auna for a meeting. Top-level classified. Both of you are required to attend — I've signed the confidentiality agreement."

"'Top-Level Classified Meeting. Subject: Response Strategy Regarding Void Army New Turret Systems (Provisional Designations VA78, VA79).'" Neni read aloud from the projection. "'Attendees: Marshals Lis, Conway, and Fujin. Firsthand witnesses: Shogun, Neni, Banshee.'"

"Commander-level personnel excluded — only the witnesses and the top brass. Pretty obvious what that means." Neni tossed the candy in her hand into the air and caught it. "They don't want this getting out. If the rest of the Alliance finds out the Void Army has stolen our stun technology, we'll lose even faster."

Shogun glanced at the bed nearby. A girl lay there, still unconscious.

"Banshee. She hasn't woken up."

The girl called Banshee looked about the same age as Hime — early twenties. Her long brown hair spilled over the edge of the bed. Her bangs were slightly disheveled, just barely covering her right eye. Her robe was torn in several places, like she'd just come through a brutal fight.

"A few hours ago I received top-priority orders to go cover Banshee's escape." Hime looked at the sleeping figure. "By the time I reached the coordinates, she was already unconscious. Every system had failed."

"I pulled her Warp Gate logs. Twenty-eight jumps. Under overwhelming enemy fire, she tried to send an alert twenty-eight times — every single one failed. Even as I was carrying her aboard, her comms system was still trying to reboot on its own." Hime's voice was quiet.

Shogun looked at Banshee in silence.

"I don't think that's the only reason she was invited to the meeting." Neni spoke up. "Human minds aren't on our level, but the ones at the top are the best of what they've got. Both Marshals must have already figured out where that technology came from."

"The brightest of the Five-Zero cohort — the only one confirmed killed in action, and the only one whose body was never recovered." Hime's expression softened into something sad.

Shogun's fist came down slowly on his knee. "Banshee's former partner," he murmured. "Aura was an ion cannon. Main Weapon was a continuous rapid-fire type — of course. It all lines up."

"Matches every one of the Void Army's new turrets, doesn't it?"

Silence settled between the three of them.

"Didn't any other patrols intercept the Void Army along the way?" Neni asked, frowning.

"The three Shipsians who tried to stop them aren't here anymore. Devout came back in two pieces — literally. Wanderer made it back with just a head. They were the lucky ones. Yaxta flew straight into a Doomsday Laser at point-blank range. Nothing left."

Hime tapped the holographic display. Three greyed-out portraits appeared, stark against the pale blue background.

"Those Void Army bastards…"

The candy still in Neni's hand let out a sharp crack as her fist closed around it, crushing it to powder.

Hime looked down. Neni silently tipped the fragments into her mouth. A few pieces fell to the floor and were swept away by a small cleaning drone that bumped against her foot a couple of times in the process.

A faint sweetness spread across her tongue.

"Blight Chrysanthemum."
