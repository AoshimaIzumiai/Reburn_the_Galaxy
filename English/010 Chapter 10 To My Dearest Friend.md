### Alliance Year 47, October 2

**To: Phoenix**

Hey Phoenix!

I just finished my exams and I'm sitting at a window terminal in the communications office, typing this out on the clunkiest keyboard I've ever touched. Civilian interstellar mail is something else — the staff here said it takes at least two weeks to reach you, maybe a month if we're unlucky. By the time you read this, I'll probably already have my results back. But I'm confident — full marks shouldn't be a problem.

The last question on the math exam was a geometry proof. I still remember the problem, so I've attached it at the end of this message! It's a tricky one, but I know you'll figure it out. Honestly, without you competing with me for first place, exams just aren't as fun anymore.

Have you started training yet? I'm dying to know! Is it physics? Military theory? Or did they throw you straight into combat drills? You have to tell me everything!

Write back soon!

Your friend,
Disar

### Alliance Year 47, October 25

**To: Disar**

DISAR!!

I finally got your email this morning!! You have no idea — all communications here are incredibly strict. My personal terminal can't receive anything directly; I have to wait for a special department to forward it to me. Sending a reply is even more complicated. I spent half the day running through the process just to get this one message out. But seeing your words made it completely worth it!

I solved the math problem you attached! I drew so many construction lines before I figured it out — and then after I finished, I realized there was a much simpler approach. I know you would have spotted it immediately.

The coursework here is *hard*. Guess what I'm studying? Electrical engineering and aerodynamics. University-level stuff. The instructors say it's all preparation for Shipsian combat. It's exhausting, but I'm slowly keeping up. I really wish I could mail you the textbooks so we could study together — but that's apparently not allowed.

Oh, and something kind of incredible: this year, I'm the only new Shipsian identified across the entire galaxy. So Marshal Fujin is teaching me personally — even history class. Sounds impressive, right? It's actually more like a very elegant form of torture. One-on-one lessons, three instructors and ten cameras watching my every move. The student dorms are completely empty except for me. I miss studying side by side with you so much.

But getting your email makes me feel a lot less alone. Thank you for writing to me. Even across all this distance, it feels like you're right here.

Miss you! Write back soon!

Your friend,
Phoenix

### Alliance Year 47, November 14

**To: Disar**

Disar!

You have no idea how lucky the timing was — I sent you a message, and your new email arrived the very next day! Thanks for letting me know about my parents!

The staff here told me that because I'm a Shipsian, my parents are under Alliance protection and have been relocated to Arcology. Yes, that jungle planet — apparently it's a popular vacation spot for the wealthy. If you ever visit, feel free to drop by and see them!

I've also learned more about how Shipsian warriors are classified. There are three types: frontline combat, logistics and support, and those integrated into the human military. Apparently at eighteen, all of us go through a major evaluation — pass, and you qualify for the first two categories; fail, and you get reassigned. That's terrifying! But I'm going to pass. And you have to keep working hard too — we promised we'd meet again at the Prometheus Array!

I've also been getting to know some of the senior Shipsians here. There's one who sells strawberry ice cream in the dorm lobby — goes by "Yoth-Hola." There's another who stands at the cafeteria entrance making sure everyone eats properly — they call him Reaper. And then there's EX04239, who always wears a mask and a white exoskeleton. They're all so fascinating!

Oh no, I've hit the size limit — I'll have to save the rest for next time! Write back soon!

Your friend,
Phoenix

I'm so jealous that you've already made so many Shipsian friends at the base! But don't worry about me — I'll work hard too. My parents mentioned we might be moving somewhere with better schools, which would give me a better shot at the Alliance Army entrance exams. I'm really grateful they support my goals. It gives me so much confidence. Trust me!

If the move goes through, I'll send you my new address right away. Don't forget to update your contact list!

Missing you,
Disar

### Alliance Year 47, December 10

**To: Phoenix**

Hey Phoenix!

Something incredibly embarrassing happened at school yesterday. During PE, I was running too fast and crashed straight into a delivery robot on the track. Parts went everywhere. The whole class was laughing. I wanted to disappear. The teacher made me reassemble the robot as punishment — I was at it until midnight before I got it back together.

You're definitely going to laugh at me, aren't you? Fine. But just you wait — I'm going to outscore you on the next exam and prove I'm not someone to underestimate!

Honestly though, every time something embarrassing happens, I really wish you were there. Because I know you'd smile and hand me a tissue — not laugh until you fell over.

Write back soon! Miss you!

Your friend,
Disar

Marshal Fujin arranged it himself — he mentioned his hometown is on an island nation on Earth. It's my first Christmas ever, and I'm planning to eat as much Earth food as I possibly can!

### Alliance Year 48, January 25

**To: Phoenix**

Dear Phoenix,

I'm sorry it took me so long to reply. My final exam results were… not good. My parents said it's because I kept going to the communications office and falling behind on studying. I know I should have worked harder. But back then, just knowing I might get a message from you made everything feel worth it — and now I can see that was selfish of me.

There's something else I need to tell you. We've just transferred to a new school. It has a closed-campus policy — students board on-site, and contact with family and the outside world is strictly limited. On top of that, the cost of sending a message from here to the Prometheus Array has gone up significantly, and my parents say they can no longer afford to cover frequent correspondence. Most importantly, they've told me I'm no longer permitted to write to you privately.

So starting now, I won't be able to reply the way I used to. Any letters you send will go through my parents first. I'll try to ask them to pass your news along to me, but I can't promise they'll send my replies back. You may not hear from me for a while — possibly a long while. I'm sorry. That makes me genuinely sad.

Please don't stop writing because of me. Every letter you've sent is something I've read over and over again, tucked under my blanket in the dark. I'll do my best to find the right moment to change things — but it may take time.

See you at graduation. Don't forget me.

I'll miss you very, very much,
Disar

### Alliance Year 48, February 19

**To: Disar**

Disar!

I just found out — I don't get summer or winter breaks!!

Marshal Fujin told me that because Shipsians are so rare, the galaxy's battlefronts are critically short on strength. We finish theoretical studies at sixteen and go straight into two years of simulation training. By the time you sit your Alliance Army entrance exams, I'll probably already be a full warrior — as long as I graduate on schedule!

I really want to go somewhere outside the Prometheus Array. Especially Arcology. That jungle planet must be so beautiful…

Hope you're doing well!

Waiting for your reply,
Phoenix

### Alliance Year 48, March 4

**To: Disar**

Long time no write!

School must have started back up for you, right? How was the break? Mine wasn't exactly restful. I'm guessing yours wasn't either, with the closed-campus policy — probably stuck at school the whole time. That sounds just as boring as being here.

The instructors say the difficulty is going up. There's only one student — me — but nine different subject teachers taking turns watching my every move. And every single one of them outranks me by a terrifying margin. There is absolutely no slacking off.

I really want to go outside and do something fun…

Missing you,
Phoenix

### Alliance Year 48, September 15

**To: Disar**

Hi Disar.

Something mildly catastrophic happened during training today — I tripped over myself inside the vacuum adaptation chamber. You know how once you start moving in zero gravity you just can't stop? I ended up bouncing around the whole chamber, hitting the walls several times before I finally stabilized. And the entire time, ten instructors and ten cameras had a front-row seat to the whole thing. I wanted to find a hole and disappear into it.

If you were here, you'd be laughing until your stomach hurt, wouldn't you?

Sometimes I wish the instructors here weren't so serious all the time. I just want someone my own age to talk to. But there's no one like that here. Just me.

Your friend,
Phoenix

### Alliance Year 49, January 1

**To: Disar**

Happy New Year!

I went to a Christmas party a few days ago — my second one. It was considerably more boring than last year. The person running it was a very dull engineer, and hardly any Shipsians showed up.

So I made a decision. After dinner, I went out to the lawn, lay down with an ice cream, and looked up at the stars by myself.

Ceres Major doesn't glow from here. But I know you're somewhere out there in that sea of stars. Maybe you're looking out your window at the stars right now too? When will we see each other again? Maybe that's too much to ask.

Eighteen is getting closer. We'll meet again — I know it. Like last night's meteor shower, light from two different worlds crossing paths in the same sky.

Tonight I'm being a little reckless. I'm going to sleep out here on this lawn. Even if I get in trouble. Even if I catch a cold. I just want to lie here and let the galaxy look down at me for a while, and dream.

Your friend,
Phoenix

### Alliance Year 51, April 20

**To: Disar**

Hi Disar.

A rare day off today, so I spent the whole thing thinking about what I wanted to share with you. I wrote a very long letter — then deleted two-thirds of it. Not because of the size limit. I just worried that writing too much would only make the distance feel greater.

Tonight the instructors called a surprise drill out of nowhere. By the time it was over it was deep into the night, and walking back I looked up and saw the stars again. I had this thought: if I wrote my name on a star, would you happen to look up and see it? When I become a warrior and get sent on missions to uninhabited planets, I'll arrange a hundred lights to spell out "Phoenix" — who knows, maybe technology will make telescopes powerful enough to read it by then.

I really want to talk to you. To tell you everything I've seen these past months.

— The rest of this letter is going into the recycling bin.

Your friend,
Phoenix

It's late. Goodnight. Hope you're well.

Your friend,
Phoenix
