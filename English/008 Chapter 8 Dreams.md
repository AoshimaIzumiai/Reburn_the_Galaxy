# Chapter 8: Dreams

## 1

Proxymar tipped his hat slightly in greeting.

"Good evening, sir!" Disar snapped to attention first, a little flustered, then nudged Phoenix's hand when she noticed her still staring blankly at Proxymar.

"Oh — good evening!" Phoenix snapped back to reality, caught Disar already standing at attention out of the corner of her eye, and quickly straightened up as well.

Proxymar laughed warmly and rested a hand on each of their shoulders. "No need for formalities, kids. You're not Alliance soldiers — you don't have to salute us."

Phoenix felt the gentle pressure on her shoulder and straightened up, meeting Proxymar's gaze.

"It's a great honor to meet you, Commander Proxymar!" Disar said, spine perfectly straight. Phoenix, by comparison, looked entirely relaxed.

Proxymar raised his left arm and tapped his wrist twice with his right hand. A small wristband device activated, projecting a pale blue holographic screen.

"Per regulations, non-Alliance personnel may only stay on base for one day. It's already late at night here on the Prometheus Array, so your blood tests have been scheduled for tomorrow morning." He explained, then added, "I'm here to show you both to your quarters — I trust you can read the Galactic Common Script."

The projection displayed a map. Rows of Galactic Common Script, marked with arrows, pointed toward a guest room. The characters — a blend of scripts from across the major planets — were easy enough for Disar to pick out her own name.

"Disar… Guest Reception Wing…" Phoenix read quietly.

Disar studied the map carefully. Every facility was labeled in multiple languages with impressive detail — but nowhere among those characters could she find anything meaning "Phoenix." She thought she must have missed it, rubbed her eyes, and looked again. Still nothing.

"Wait — what about me?" Phoenix murmured to herself.

Disar had already reached the same conclusion. "Phoenix isn't staying here?"

"Regrettably, the base has had an unusually high number of visitors lately, and only one single-occupancy room remains in the Guest Reception Wing."

"Oh…" Phoenix glanced away, a little embarrassed. "Disar and I can share — sorry for the trouble…"

Proxymar looked at Phoenix and continued: "Fortunately, one of our Shipsian warriors has offered to share their quarters. It's not ideal, but Miss Phoenix will be spending the night in the Shipsian residential wing."

— *Where?*

Phoenix froze. She stared straight at Proxymar.

The *Shipsian* residential wing?

Staying with *active Shipsian warriors*?!

"Me?"

"That's right."

"*Really?!*"

She couldn't quite believe it, and the words came out louder than she intended.

Proxymar smiled and nodded.

Phoenix nearly fainted from excitement. She had already thought that meeting so many Shipsians — even spending time with them — was the luckiest thing that could ever happen to her. For ordinary people, these warriors only ever appeared in news headlines or on television.

To be this close to them, like this — it was as if she'd spent every last drop of luck she'd ever have.

"PHOENIX, you are so *lucky*!!"

Disar launched herself at Phoenix and ruffled her hair into a mess.

"I'm so jealous — I want to stay with the Shipsian warriors too!"

"Okay, okay, stop messing up my hair!"

"You're burning through ten lifetimes of luck right now!"

While the two of them were laughing and shoving each other, Proxymar raised a hand for quiet.

"You may not feel it, but it's the middle of the night on base. Alliance protocol requires keeping noise to a minimum during these hours." He lowered his voice and showed them his watch — the hands pointed to one in the morning.

"Hime should be here by now — ah, perfect timing."

Phoenix felt a faint vibration in her eardrums and instinctively traced it to its source.

*Thrusters?*

A platinum figure rose smoothly from below the staircase. Hime was flying straight up along the outside of the stairs, her skirt dancing in the air current stirred by her leg thrusters.

"My apologies — something came up at the last moment. I asked Commander Proxymar to wait here with you."

Proxymar watched Hime land beside the two girls and finish her explanation, then said with a teasing lilt: "What is it with everyone refusing to use the exterior staircase lately?"

"Because I have friends here who want to see a little more of what high-tech looks like in action." Hime said, and winked at the two kids beside her.

"Can thruster sounds really be that quiet?" Phoenix asked, slightly off-topic, then got poked in the arm by Disar and scratched her head to signal she hadn't meant to interrupt.

"Right then, Hime — as arranged, you'll take Miss Disar to the Guest Reception Wing." Proxymar shifted his gaze to Phoenix. "I'll take Miss Phoenix to the Shipsian residential wing."

*Hm?*

Something about that felt odd to Phoenix, though she couldn't say what. She nodded anyway.

"No time to waste — let's move."

## 2

After saying goodbye to Disar and Hime, Phoenix walked in silence beside Proxymar, heading toward the Shipsian residential wing.

She had to admit — after everything that had happened, she was tired.

It had only been half a day since the moment she first heard the alarm blaring through the passenger vessel. In those few short hours, she had lived through more than most people would in a lifetime.

Even now, thinking back on it, she still couldn't believe she'd had that kind of nerve — pulling the cannon's firing handles while staring down a laser that could have turned her to ash in a second.

The time she'd spent unconscious on the patrol vessel hadn't restored her energy the way she'd hoped. Physical and mental exhaustion pressed down on her at once. Phoenix walked with her head low, not saying a word.

The Alliance base was quiet at this hour. When they'd arrived, the port had been alive with noise and motion. Now, barely an hour later, the whole world seemed to have hit mute. No transport vehicles, no workers crossing the floor, no hum of drones overhead.

She caught a glimpse of one of the large clocks mounted everywhere on the walls. The hands pointed to a section marked in blue, with a small legend beside it: *Silent Rest Period*.

So they shut everything down to make sure Alliance personnel could sleep.

It really was quiet enough to make you drowsy.

Phoenix yawned, then shook her head to clear it.

"Phoenix."

Proxymar spoke suddenly, breaking the stillness of the empty base.

"Y-yes, sir?" Phoenix answered, a little startled and uncertain.

*Why is the Commander calling my name? Is this about the attack earlier? But I already told them everything in the debrief.* Her heart picked up speed on its own.

"Tired?"

"Huh?"

Phoenix blinked, slow to process.

Proxymar smiled and held his watch up in front of her.

The small screen reflected a face back at her.

Phoenix was surprised — because the face looking back at her wore an exhaustion she hadn't realized was there. Even as her expression shifted to surprise, the stiffness in her features gave away just how worn out she really was.

"Sorry, sir — I don't know why I suddenly feel like I could fall asleep…"

"We still have a bit of a walk ahead. Would you mind if I carried you?"

"No, no, no — please don't, I can walk fine on my own."

Phoenix went rigid and waved her hands frantically.

"Then let's talk about something — keep you awake." Proxymar lowered his arm and looked up at the space above them. "Let me think… what do kids your age usually like to talk about… How about dreams? What do you want to do with your life?"

*Dreams?*

The answer came to her immediately.

"I… I want to become a Shipsian warrior."

"That tone won't do."

Phoenix blinked — then caught herself.

"I want to become a Shipsian warrior!"

Proxymar smiled. "And why is that? From what I hear, your grades are excellent — and after what happened today, the Alliance will almost certainly offer you certain privileges. Getting into the Alliance Army won't be difficult. Once you're in, you could be a researcher, a commander. Why does it have to be a warrior?"

Phoenix didn't hesitate.

"Because I want to be someone else's hope."

Proxymar went still. That was clearly not the answer he'd expected. His mouth opened slightly — but no sound came out.

Phoenix seemed to remember something and added: "Disar thinks the same way — we've known each other since we were little. She's so much better than me at everything. I've always had a feeling she's a Shipsian."

Even without the sound of machinery, the lights of the Galactic Alliance Army base burned through the night. Cold white light blanketed the metal ground and scattered softly onto the girl walking through it. The tips of her sky-blue hair lifted in the small breeze of her own movement, and her gold eyes caught the light — bright and alive, like a pair of canaries bathed in sunlight. Like the girl she had just been.

Proxymar didn't answer right away. His footsteps slowed for a moment. He looked ahead for a long time before he finally spoke.

"…Is that so." His tone was flat — nothing like the easy warmth he'd had when he first brought up the subject. There was something in it that wasn't hard to read as disappointment. Then, almost to himself, he added quietly: "So it really is… fate."

He said nothing more after that, and Phoenix gradually settled back into silence too.

Talking had helped with the drowsiness — but the moment she stopped thinking and answering, the sleepiness came rolling back in, especially now that both she and the Commander had gone quiet.

The tiredness swept over her again.

Something green seemed to flash past at the edge of her vision. She glanced up — but there was nothing there.

*Maybe I really do need to sleep right now. I'm already seeing things.* Phoenix thought.

Then she noticed Proxymar slow his pace — just slightly.

Her ears caught something. A sound, sharp and wrong.

A point of purple entered her field of vision.

And then — in an instant —

— it came tearing through the air.

## 3

"— Get down!"

Proxymar's shout cracked through the air. In the span of a blink, the Lightning Conduit deployed across his back. Lightning coiled and crackled, a bolt of thunder surging up his arm and launching forward — colliding head-on with a streak of dark violet-crimson blade light that had come slicing through the dark.

*BOOM —*

The explosion detonated less than ten meters from Phoenix.

Her body locked up. Every instinct screamed at her to run, but her legs refused to move.

*Call for help. Scream. Someone will hear.*

She tried. Her throat produced nothing.

*What's happening? What is this?*

*Why can't I move?*

*Why can't I make a sound?*

*Why? Why?*

"Didn't expect the famous new Commander to be here as well. My mistake."

A middle-aged man stepped out from the shadows, a tattered cloak hanging from his shoulders. His hat couldn't contain the yellow-green hair spilling out beneath it, and gold eyes gleamed from under the brim.

The explosion must have triggered a sensor. A piercing alarm began to wail.

"Tch. Annoying."

The man raised one hand, fingers pressed flat and straight like a blade, and flicked his wrist. A rectangular wave of force shot out and sliced the alarm unit clean in two.

Proxymar's expression hardened. He stared at the intruder with cold, fixed eyes.

"Trin. I. Ty."

He bit out the name one syllable at a time.

"A wanted criminal — how did you get in here?"

"Credit goes to the ones who've seen things clearly. Same as me."

Trinity extended one hand. A pale violet light swirled at his fingertips, his tone edged with contempt.

"You're aware that appearing here gives me the right to put you down on the spot."

Proxymar's voice dropped low. He stepped in front of Phoenix, who still couldn't move.

*Why? Why?*

The fearlessness she'd felt staring down the Doomsday Laser — that complete disregard for her own life — was nowhere to be found right now.

*Why is it like this?*

Phoenix was rooted to the ground. Only the trembling of her eyes betrayed that she was still alive.

"Put me down? You think you have what it takes, kid?" Trinity scoffed. The blue point of light at his chest blazed bright.

"What do you want, Ana-Shipsian?"

"You still don't know?"

Trinity's expression twisted into something savage. He raised his arm, and a deep crimson light began to arc along the outside of it — his Main Weapon.

"Hand her over —"

Phoenix suddenly realized that his gaze — the kind that felt like it could tear through anything — had landed directly on her.

"— **Phoenix II**."

## 4

The lightning coils along Proxymar's Lightning Conduit surged with greater intensity.

"Not a chance!"

"You're not in my league, kid!"

Lightning and blade light both began charging at once, as if a single moment was all it would take before they collided again. Phoenix stared at the scene in front of her in horror, desperately trying to back away from a battlefield that felt like it could swallow her whole at any second — but her body had gone completely offline, refusing every command she gave it.

Something else was behind her. She held her breath. The tension reached a peak she had never felt before.

She wanted to turn around and see who it was. She prayed it was Hime, or Aries, or Starless — even Nimbus. Faces of Shipsians she didn't know flashed through her mind, ones she'd seen in newspapers and on television. Her thoughts were dissolving into chaos.

*Please — whoever you are — help me —*

"Both of you. Stand down."

Phoenix heard a *clack-clack* sound directly above her head.

A man stepped past her. The source of the sound was the grey-white light machine gun mounted on his shoulder, its holographic targeting reticle already projected and live.

"Trinity. That's enough."

The grey-haired man looked up at Trinity with steady eyes.

"Long time no see, Corsair."

Trinity's brow furrowed as he said the name.

The man called Corsair wore white-gold clothing layered with ammunition of every kind, each round seemingly engraved with something. A captain's hat sat on his head, the Major insignia on its side catching the light. Phoenix noticed the long scar running down his left eye — from his forehead all the way to his cheek.

"Running into each other like this, old friend." Corsair sighed. "Not exactly how I'd have wanted it."

"Hmph." Trinity kept his arm raised, his voice dripping with disdain. "You're one of the people who knows the truth better than almost anyone! Corsair — the chance to save her, to save *all of us*, is right here in front of you!"

Corsair's brow stayed tight. The light machine gun on his shoulder let out a low hum.

"You still don't get it, do you? That person is gone. She has her own will now. Whatever happened before has nothing to do with her." 

"That's rich, Corsair." Trinity held his ground, looking down at him with cold, superior eyes. "You can't even bring yourself to say her name to my face?"

*What? Why? How did it come to this? What are they all talking about?*

Fear and confusion hit Phoenix at the same time. She didn't understand any of what was happening — but she understood clearly enough that these people had come here because of *her*. And in a standoff this close to igniting, there was a very real chance she'd end up as collateral.

"Proxymar. Get her out."

Corsair kept his eyes on Trinity, voice low and steady.

Proxymar didn't waste a fraction of a second. The mechanical sound of his Main Weapon splitting apart cut through the silence, the fishbone structure dissolving into countless small components that folded away beneath his coat.

"Phoenix — hold on!"

He spun around, grabbed her by the arm — she was still completely frozen — and hauled her off the ground with one arm, swinging her onto his back.

The sudden jolt was enough to bring some feeling back into her body. Phoenix gripped his shoulders with everything she had, fingers digging in to keep herself from falling.

"Don't even think about it!"

Before the words were out, Trinity vanished from where he stood. A blinding green light flared — and Phoenix saw him reappear directly above her head.

Purple light flashed.

Not Trinity's crimson blade wave. Something else entirely.

Space seemed to freeze for just an instant. A massive pillar of violet light slammed down and sealed off Trinity's path completely.

Proxymar seized the opening. His leg thrusters fired at full power, and he shot away into the distance with Phoenix on his back.

*What just happened —*

The wind screamed past her ears. Phoenix felt her mind going dark at the edges.

Just before consciousness slipped away entirely, she heard a furious roar from somewhere behind her.

"*Mirage* — !!"

## 5

Darkness.

"Beep. Beep."

"Rise…"

"Beep. Beep."

"Hear…"

"Beep. Beep."

"Wake…"

"Beep —"

"Phoenix?"

"Ah!"

Phoenix jolted upright in bed with a cry.

"Bad dream?"

Phoenix suddenly realized she was lying in a soft single bed. The steady beeping was coming from an alarm clock.

"I… um…"

She looked up. A young woman was standing at her bedside.

"You were saying things like 'don't kill me' and 'help' in your sleep. Are you okay…?"

Phoenix took in the person in front of her. From her appearance, she looked to be around twenty. Her brown hair was long enough that the ends nearly reached her knees, and her bangs were slightly disheveled, just barely covering her right eye. The left eye that showed carried a quiet, indefinable sadness.

"Sorry… um… could you tell me what happened last night?"

"You were already asleep when you arrived at the dorm. Commander Proxymar carried you back." The young woman answered in a voice that was gentle but carried a certain weight. "You can call me Banshee."

"Banshee — ma'am." Phoenix added a respectful suffix after the name, not entirely sure if it was appropriate.

*Last night…*

*What happened last night, exactly?*

Phoenix blinked.

She remembered saying goodbye to Disar and setting off with Proxymar toward the Shipsian residential wing.

After that…

After that, she'd started feeling drowsy somewhere along the way.

And then…

That was it.

"Oh!"

Something clicked, and Phoenix slapped both hands over her face.

*I fell asleep while walking?*

*In front of a Galactic Alliance Army Commander?*

*And he carried me back?!*

*Please tell me I didn't talk in my sleep the whole way.*

Phoenix pinched herself in disbelief.

"Don't worry about it. The nightmares are probably residual effects from facing the Doomsday Laser head-on." Banshee's lips curved slightly — something like a smile, though it carried a trace of bitterness.

"I had a strange dream just now — sorry about that, Miss Banshee…" Phoenix scratched her head to cover her embarrassment. "I dreamed someone was trying to kill me. Though honestly, I've been having weird dreams for a while now."

*That explains it.* In the dream she couldn't move and couldn't scream — classic nightmare logic. Her brain's dream department was malfunctioning again.

Phoenix tapped her own forehead.

"The test…" Banshee smiled and held out a towel. "It's almost time. Here."

Phoenix nodded and reached out, taking the warmth from her hands.
