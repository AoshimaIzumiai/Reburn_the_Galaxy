# Chapter 1: Prologue — Phoenix
## 1

Darkness.

"Beep. Beep."

"Language module — nominal."

"Beep. Beep."

"Motor module nominal. Sensory functions nominal."

"Beep. Beep."

"Emotional module nominal."

"Beep—"

"Project Reburn… initiated."

She tried to open her eyes. The first thing that hit her was a blinding white light.
Then dizziness — a wave of it — followed by an unbearable ringing in her ears.
After that, silence.


## 2
"Ding—"

A hand shot out and smacked the blaring alarm clock, silencing the sound that had torn through her dream.
The girl sat up in bed, running a hand through her messy sky-blue hair.
She opened her deep golden eyes lazily, muttering softly about the dream that had been haunting her lately.

"Why is it always that dream…"

"What were they even saying?"

She got dressed and gathered her long hair into a loose, low ponytail that fell in a single thin strand. Then she pulled back the curtains. Sunlight squeezed in through the narrow gaps between the apartment buildings, struggling to reach her room. She stretched, looking down at the busy people below.

"Good morning, Ceres Major."

But today was different. The market below wasn't nearly as crowded as usual.

"What's going on…"

Something clicked. She froze — then let out a startled gasp. She grabbed her jacket from the chair behind her and bolted out the door.

"Phoenix, come eat your—"

"Mom, I'll eat when I get back!"

"Where are you going?"

"The award ceremony's starting!"

The girl named Phoenix threw on her jacket, gave a quick wave to her mother standing there with a piece of bread, and dashed out the front door without looking back.

## 3
"Excuse me — pardon me, thank you…"

Phoenix wove through the crowd.
This was the award ceremony for the person she admired most in the world. How could she be late?
Curse that dream!
She kept grumbling internally as she pushed her way through the gaps in the crowd, head down, focused on nothing else.

Thud!

"Oof!"

She walked straight into the back of a tall man.

"Oh…" She rubbed her forehead and looked up at the broad figure in front of her. "Sorry about that."

"My apologies, miss."

The man in the blue suit tipped his hat in apology.

"Don't worry about it." Phoenix was already eager to get moving. She smiled, waved him off, and slipped back into the crowd.

But the man stood frozen the moment he saw her face. By the time he came back to himself, she had already disappeared.

"That was…"

"Aries, what's wrong?"

"Nothing, Starless. I'm fine."

He stared in the direction Phoenix had gone.

"I bumped into a young woman just now."

"Come on, let's go. Colonel Proxymar's ceremony is about to start."

Starless tugged at Aries's sleeve, but he didn't move. When Starless turned to press him, he caught the look in Aries's eyes — unmistakably stunned.

"Aries, what is it?"

"That face… it looked familiar. Like I've seen it somewhere before."

## 4
The girl caught the whispers drifting through the audience.

"I heard Marshal Fujin is here today?"

"That's right — he's standing over there, look!"

"This will go down in history. Two generations of heroes on the same stage."

The emcee was recounting the hero's battle in a voice full of energy. From the moment the enemy ships were spotted, to the lone charge into their formation — the precision of the missile strikes was described as though divinely guided, and the massive bomb had sounded all the more formidable for it.
The speakers were loud. She could feel the sound in her chest.
5
By the time Phoenix finally pushed her way to the front of the crowd, the lights around the stage suddenly blazed to life.

"Please welcome — the hero who saved Ceres Major from the brink of destruction — Colonel Proxymar!!"

The crowd erupted. Military caps flew into the air in celebration.
Through the gaps in the crowd, she finally caught a glimpse of the middle-aged man on the stage.
Proxymar wore a deep brown coat, his short sandy hair neat and sharp. He gave a wave, and the crowd roared again.
"Hero." "Fearless warrior." "The Colonel." The people of Ceres Major were shouting over each other as reporters' cameras flashed without pause.

But Phoenix just stared blankly at the figure on stage.

From below the steps, a man with long hair walked slowly up to the stage. His violet robes drifted around him as if stirred by a wind that wasn't there, and in his hands rested a single medal.
This medal belonged to a hero.

"Marshal Fujin."

Proxymar bowed toward the Marshal.

"This is what you have earned, Colonel." The Marshal smiled gently, and pinned the medal to Proxymar's chest.

Fujin turned to face the crowd and raised the microphone. The roaring audience fell still in an instant.

"The man standing beside me is Colonel Proxymar — who, not long ago, single-handedly dismantled an enemy strike force during an invasion, and in doing so, disrupted the enemy's assault on this planet and saved every soul on it.

"Colonel Proxymar is a model for every soldier in the Galactic Alliance Army. On behalf of the Galactic Alliance Army, I am proud to present this medal to this brave Shipsian warrior.

"From this moment forward, Proxymar is promoted to Commander. It is my hope that in the battles ahead, more soldiers — braver soldiers — will stand up and fight for the peace and safety of this galaxy."

The applause was thunderous. Phoenix's eyes shimmered.

"I want to become… a hero like that."

## 5
"Well, well. Even the esteemed Marshal Fujin finds time for leisure — to what do I owe the honor of this visit?"

The door creaked open. A shaft of light cut into the dim, run-down room.
Dust motes caught in the light swirled visibly through the air, followed by the dull thud of something heavy hitting the floor in a corner of the room.

"Marshal, I sincerely hope you shut down your olfactory module before stepping in here. This place is considerably filthier than any Alliance Army office. Mine, of course, broke long ago."

A middle-aged man with yellow-green hair sat in a chair that had seen better days. The coffee table in front of him was thick with cobwebs.
He looked toward the door at Fujin — who, for once, was dressed in plain clothes, with a hat pulled low over his face.

"It's been a while, old friend. You still recognized me."

"My radar isn't broken. You were standing outside the door, and I already knew it was you." The man gave a cold laugh. "So you still remember me. Color me touched."

"What — can't I come see an old friend? You haven't cleaned this place since back then, have you." Fujin closed the door and took off his hat. "Trinity."

"That old friend of yours who loved a clean room died in Blight."

Trinity lifted a bottle of something murky from the table.

"Vintage stuff. Care for a drink?"

"I'm not here to reminisce, Trinity."

He set the bottle down, rolled his shoulders, cleared his throat, and said drily: "Oh? The Marshal's come for a fight, then? My Chrono Field is overdue for a tune-up, but the Darkfire Blast still works."

"Trinity. I'm being serious."

"Then spit it out."

"…"

Trinity's body gave an involuntary shudder when he heard those words.

"Really…? Fujin, you're not lying to me?"

"Every word is true."

"Fine. Good. Good for the Galactic Alliance Army. Good for humanity. And good for this so-called 'Project Reburn…"

"What are you going to do?"

"Me? I'm going to go sort things out, obviously."

"Don't do anything reckless. Don't forget you're still on humanity's wanted list."

"Relax."

"Then I'll take my leave."

"Wait — Fujin. Promise me one thing."

"What?"

"This time… don't let her go down the same road again."

"But isn't that the whole point of Project Reburn?"

"If it really comes to that, Fujin — I might just go down that same road myself."

"Everything has changed. You have. So have I. And I believe… so has she."

## 6
Watching his old friend leave, Trinity slumped back into his chair. Those words circled in his mind, over and over—

"*I saw Phoenix.*"
