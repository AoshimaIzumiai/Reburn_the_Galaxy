# Chapter 4: Ablaze

## 1

The voice coming from the medal was slightly distorted—but it brought an overwhelming sense of reassurance. Calling it a lifeline wouldn’t be an exaggeration.

Disar held the medal in both hands, still recovering from the pain, unable to respond immediately.

The sound of escape pods detaching echoed faintly from the corridor.

“Recruit? Do you copy?”

“Y-Yes!”

Phoenix kicked off the wall, drifting in zero gravity toward Disar, answering quickly.

“Report the situation immediately.”

Phoenix glanced out the window at once. Several Void Army ships streaked across space, their thrusters leaving curved trails behind.

“Actually—”

Disar tried to explain that they weren’t recruits—just two student passengers under attack—but Phoenix cut in first.

“Passenger vessel under attack. Limited single-side visibility: one medium-class, four small-class, approximately ten Sparrow-class equipped with cluster-guided multi-warheads. Laser and Speed Laser Turrets confirmed. Enemy reinforcements still arriving.”

Disar stared at Phoenix in shock—not because she knew the terminology.

Phoenix, you’re really going to get us treated like soldiers—!

“Copy. Your coordinates are confirmed. Nearest Alliance forces are en route. Status of your defenses?”

Phoenix turned slightly—but Disar answered first:

“Vessel ID: Dovacoon 194. Shield fully destroyed. Auto cannons destroyed. One heavy machine gun moderately damaged, one lightly damaged.”

They exchanged a look.

Disar saw determination in Phoenix’s eyes—and Phoenix saw the same in hers.

“Vessel status confirmed. Proceed immediately to the lightly damaged heavy machine gun and switch to manual control. Do everything you can to engage the enemy and keep them away from the escape pods. Move fifteen meters toward the front and open the emergency valve on the right to access the lift. Communication ending—signal too long risks detection.”

The worst-case scenario Disar had feared had arrived.

They weren’t soldiers. They were just two thirteen-year-old students.

Attacking meant being attacked in return. One mistake—and they’d be killed on the spot.

Soldiers die on the battlefield.

And since they never clarified their identity—they were being treated as soldiers.

Phoenix looked at the medal in her hand, then at the lasers flashing outside.

“Understood.”

Disar knew she would say that.

Phoenix wanted to be a hero.

And wasn’t she the same?

Disar grabbed Phoenix’s wrist. Their trembling hands steadied slightly.

“Together.”


## 2

They rushed through the corridor, alarms blaring. Debris littered the floor. Outside, flashes of red and purple lasers streaked past, along with explosions from enemy ships being hit.

“Phoenix, here!”

Disar spotted a blinking red light near the right wall.

“Move these boxes!”

They cleared the luggage blocking the area, revealing a sealed hatch labeled:

**Heavy Machine Gun Control Chamber**

Phoenix grabbed the handle and pulled—but it wouldn’t budge.

“Why is it so tight—”

BOOM—

Another explosion rocked the ship. The shockwave tilted the vessel, sending them tumbling backward—and Phoenix yanked the hatch open in the process.

When things stabilized, Disar leaned in. A vertical ladder led downward into darkness. The lights were dead—the bottom couldn’t even be seen.

“I’ll go first.”

Phoenix hung the silent medal around her neck and climbed down.

“Be careful.”

Clang. Clang. Clang.

Her footsteps echoed.

When she reached the bottom, she realized she stood before a spherical glass wall. Beyond it—space, stars, the ruined Warp Gate, and chaotic enemy movements.

This was the control chamber.

A cramped spherical space. Only one seat—but enough room for another person to stand. Outside, the gun barrel extended into deep space.

The holographic display read: **Automatic System Failure**.

Phoenix quickly found the manual control switch—and pressed it.

“Manual system activated. Shield online. Weapons connected. Push lever to enter combat mode.”

The display changed. A targeting reticle appeared.

Phoenix called Disar down. Soon, Disar joined her.

Phoenix took the seat. Disar crouched behind, gripping the backrest.

“I’m pushing the lever. Ready?”

“We’re thirteen! Isn’t it a bit early to go to war and kill people?!”

“Are you scared?”

“…Maybe—but I’m not backing down!”

Phoenix smiled—and pushed the lever forward.

BOOM—

The heavy machine gun beneath the ship powered up. The spherical turret slid along its track, locking into position.

Two small enemy ships turned toward them.

Inside the chamber, Phoenix gripped the controls.

“Twin light machine guns loaded. 300 rounds remaining!”

Disar reported loudly.

Phoenix aligned the reticle.

“This feels like those arcade shooting games,” Disar said, tapping her shoulder.

“Then I’m kind of excited!”

Phoenix locked onto the targets.

“Ready—”

“Fire!”


## 3

“BRRRRT—!”

Blue bullets poured out.

The recoil shook the entire chamber. The turret groaned under the strain.

Enemy ships fired back. Explosions slammed against the shield just meters away.

“Boom!”

Phoenix’s shots struck one enemy turret—it burst into flames.

“Warning: enemy laser detected.”

Disar ducked behind the seat. Phoenix didn’t stop firing.

The red laser hit.

The shield shrieked.

“Shield integrity at 19%. Recovery in 124 seconds.”

“Switch ammo! We need to finish it before the next shot!”

“Switching to single-shot shells. 20 rounds. Reloading!”

“Good.”

Phoenix re-aimed.

“Reload complete.”

BOOM—

The shot obliterated the enemy ship.

“Nice!” Disar shouted. “One more!”

Phoenix checked radar—another small one approaching.

“Reloading.”

“This thing turns so slow!”

“Rotation track is 70% damaged.”

“Damn—that’s a big weakness.”

Still—they managed.

Another shot—

Destroyed.

“Got it!”

But suddenly—

Radar blared.

A medium-class ship approached from behind.

“Damn it!”

The turret couldn’t rotate fast enough.

At ninety degrees—

Enemy fire hit.

The chamber shook violently.

“Shield at 34%!”

“Not there yet!”

The enemy repositioned—again behind them.

“Shield at 48%!”

“We can’t keep up with it!”

Phoenix strained against the controls—

Then—

A flash.


## 4

When they opened their eyes—

The attack had stopped.

“Phoenix—look!”

Outside—

A man.

Nearly 190 cm tall. His attire reminded Phoenix of Earth.

A… samurai?

He sheathed a blade. On his shoulder armor—a Major insignia of the Galactic Alliance Army.

“Phoenix—that’s a Shipsian warrior!”

She saw the golden emblem too.

A Sparrow-class enemy rushed him.

“—”

Draw. Slash. Sheathe.

In one motion.

The enemy ship was cut apart instantly.

Silence returned.

Disar slammed on the glass to get his attention.

He noticed—and moved closer, squinting inside.

“Incoming communication request from G.A.A.S. Shogun.”

Disar accepted immediately.

“Shogun connected. Was it you who sent the distress signal?”

His voice was calm, deep.

“Yes,” Disar answered.

“…How old are you?”

“Thirteen.”

“…I see.”

He was about to ask more—but destroyed another enemy without even turning.

“Do not act recklessly. Do not fire. Return inside the ship. Wait until we clear the enemies.”

“‘We’? There’s more?”

Another request came.

“From G.A.A.S. Neni.”

Phoenix accepted.

A female voice.

“Oh—it’s Shogun. You’re here too.”

“Neni. Prepare for combat. You handle medium and small targets near the ship. I’ll clear the Sparrows.”

“Copy.”

Shogun vanished in a burst of flame.

A young woman appeared—silver hair beneath a green-purple hood.

“So it really is just kids…”

Then she panicked slightly.

“Uh—hi! I’m Neni, I—”

“—Watch out!”

Phoenix shouted.

A small enemy locked onto her.

“Moonlight descends.”

Her arm device unfolded—

Transforming into a massive sniper rifle.

“Star’s trailing tail!”

Turn. Aim. Fire.

A green meteor shot out—destroying the enemy instantly.

“Was that from *The Void: Anger of the Deity*…?”

Disar muttered.

Neni froze.

“…Finally…”

“Huh?”

“Finally found fellow fans!!”

She pressed against the glass, excited.

“No one in the Alliance likes anime or visual novels—what’s your name—”

“Neni. Four o’clock, 124 meters. Medium-class turret. Take it out.”

A cold voice cut in.

“Y-Yes!”

She fled instantly.


## 5

They exhaled.

“He told us to go back inside, right?”

“Yeah… let’s go.”

Phoenix pulled the lever.

The chamber shook violently.

“Warning: track damaged. Movement halted.”

“What?!”

Again—

Same result.

Disar looked outside.

“The track… it’s broken.”

Phoenix turned.

“…Then we report it.”

She opened comms.

“Our track is broken. We can’t return.”

“Understood,” Shogun replied. “Stay silent. Avoid detection. We’ll retrieve you after the battle.”

“…Okay.”

Phoenix turned off the mic.

“Let’s trust them.”

Outside, explosions flashed—Blade Storm strikes, Vorpal Lance trails cutting through space.

“Are we just waiting here?”

“…I guess so.”

Phoenix stared into space.

Disar saw it—

Longing.

And something else.

“Phoenix…”

Beep. Beep.

“Warning: large enemy formation detected—”

They froze.


## 6

A red mass filled half the radar.

Comms activated.

“Shogun—front sector!!!”

Neni sounded shaken.

“That’s impossible… this is internal space…”

Even Shogun’s tone changed.

“I’ll report to command!”

Then—

Through gritted teeth:

“—Roc-class. Guided turrets. Rotary cannons. Doomsday Laser.”
