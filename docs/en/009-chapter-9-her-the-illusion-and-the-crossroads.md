# Chapter 9: Her, the Illusion, and the Crossroads

> "There are two tragedies in life. One is to lose your heart's desire. The other is to gain it."
>
> — George Bernard Shaw

## 1

"So… I thought this was called a *blood test*…?"

Phoenix directed the question at the camera mounted in the corner of the bright white room. She stared helplessly at the safety clamps locked around her limbs, the space filled with a steady *clack-clack* — the sound of mechanical restraints clicking shut on their own.

She was lying on something that looked uncomfortably close to a surgical table. Disar was on a parallel table nearby, in the same situation, wearing a faint look of concern. Phoenix glanced up at the tangle of wires hanging overhead, the indicator lights blinking like a miniature starfield, and felt a chill run through her.

"We followed the signs to the 'Blood Test' room… right?" Disar said, not sounding entirely convinced. She shifted slightly, trying to find a more comfortable position. Her short black hair generated static against the table surface, and the tingling sensation made her feet jerk involuntarily — her ankles knocked against the inside of the restraints with a dull thud.

Phoenix nodded as emphatically as her position allowed. "That's right. And we lay down exactly as instructed."

She'd heard about the blood test procedure from older students. The general idea was simple: a few vials of blood drawn, then go home and wait for the results — not get strapped to a surgical table like this.

Fortunately, Phoenix was very clearly aware that she was on the Prometheus Array. Otherwise, she would have been convinced she'd been lured to some lawless backwater and was about to be cut open.

"Though somehow… I feel like our lives might be in danger?"

Phoenix tried to keep her tone light, but the slight tremor in her voice gave her away.

"Scan complete. ID confirmed: G.A.A.S. Zhetass. Technician clearance verified. Door unlocked."

The door at the far end of the room opened without warning, and a flat mechanical voice drifted through. Phoenix had just opened her mouth to say something when it cut her off.

"— Of course they're not!"

The person who came through the door was not a tall, imposing technician or a doctor in a white coat. It was a small boy in an Alliance Army uniform — slight and thin, looking barely past ten years old. Nothing about him suggested "technician," except for the tool pack on his back that looked like it might topple him over.

*A kid?*

Phoenix blinked. Why was there someone even younger than her on an Alliance base?

The boy walked to the central console between the two tables. Phoenix tilted her head and caught a glimpse of the ID badge clipped to his rust-colored jacket. The name on it read: *Zhetass*.

"You're in a military base — the equipment here is built for combat operations first. The blood test machines you've seen before are far too slow for real interstellar warfare." Zhetass explained as he worked, switching on machines, turning dials, entering data on holographic screens. "The machine you're using now has been in service since Year 10 of the Alliance. Back then it identified a massive number of Shipsians in emergency conditions, and helped stabilize a defensive line that countless lives had been spent holding. It still carries enormous strategic value today — ahem, that part isn't classified."

He suddenly looked up at the ceiling, studying the cluster of wires hanging down, and rose onto his toes, apparently trying to reach a small switch dangling from above. His height, however, was not cooperating.

"Um… do you need help with—"

"【EYE】."

Disar had barely started the offer before a sharp mechanical sound cut her off.

At Zhetass's single word, the tool pack on his back let out a grinding of gears. Phoenix realized it wasn't an ordinary backpack at all — it was some kind of steel apparatus in disguise.

The "pack" detached from his back and rose into the air. Its shell split open, and like a magic trick, mechanical structures began to move all at once — extending, transforming, locking together, heavy and clanging. The two girls were too stunned to speak. They watched as the metal mass sprouted thrusters, layers of levers assembled into the shape of claws, and the whole thing took on a form that cast a genuinely unsettling shadow.

Zhetass's eyes moved, and the metal hand moved with them — pinching the wire between the tips of its claws and delivering it neatly into his hand.

"Systems nominal. Ready to proceed." Zhetass checked it over briefly, and the floating steel claw returned the wire to exactly where it had been. "By the way — this machine does considerably more than draw blood. Now. Lie still."

At the young technician's command, both girls — thoroughly shaken — lay back obediently on their tables. The machine overhead began to hum to life, and a visor-like device — something between a medical scanner and a VR headset — descended and settled over their faces.

"If you're a Shipsian, it will perform the activation surgery on the spot — awakening the Shipsian *core*. And to ensure the warrior can immediately begin training and combat readiness upon waking, it won't use anesthesia for pain suppression—"

Zhetass hadn't finished his last sentence. Phoenix didn't have time to scream. Consciousness left her.

"— It uses *hypnotic analgesia* instead."

## 2

She stood in a space of pure white.

Not the crystalline white of snow, not the clean white of paper — but a white that devoured everything, an absolute void. No shadows. No edges. Even the air had solidified into something transparent and solid. She tried to breathe, but couldn't tell whether it was her own chest rising and falling, or the entire space trembling faintly in time with her pulse.

Nothing existed here — and yet she felt as though she could sense everything.

Phoenix blinked and looked around at the boundless emptiness. A word surfaced in her mind: *white hole*.

"Hello?"

She spoke into the air. The air didn't answer. She waited. No echo came back from any wall. She counted her own heartbeat as it grew louder and louder, until it became a deafening drumbeat.

"Is anyone there?"

Of course not. She already knew the answer.

She lifted one foot and set it down gently. The moment it touched the ground, ripples spread outward. Phoenix wandered in no particular direction, but every view looked identical — she couldn't tell which way she was moving. No landmarks. Nothing to navigate by.

She knew she'd been lying on a surgical table just moments ago. Maybe this was the "consciousness space" she'd read about in science fiction. She guessed that when the test was over, everything would return to normal.

Phoenix looked down at herself. A white dress, its hem slowly dissolving at the edges into the surrounding space. She played idly with the fabric, the silk texture calming her. She shifted position, sat down cross-legged on the ground, and began humming a nursery rhyme she couldn't quite place the origin of.

*"Stars grow into crescent moons, fall into my yard and turn to watermelons;"*

Phoenix froze. She turned and looked behind her.

Because the second line hadn't come from her.

An unfamiliar woman's voice had picked up the next verse of the nameless rhyme. It moved through the air — gentle, trembling, mature, with a trace of sorrow so faint it was almost imperceptible. Like quiet grief. Like letting go.

In the pure white space, another figure had appeared. So distant — like a single accidental drop of sky-blue ink on an infinitely large white canvas. The distance between them was vast, yet the voice reached Phoenix's ears with perfect clarity.

*Someone is here.*

An inexplicable sense of relief washed over her. Phoenix opened her mouth to call out — and found she couldn't make a sound. She waved her arms urgently, but the figure gave no response.

*"Blight Chrysanthemum blooms beneath the galaxy, in winter it will die, and in spring it will grow again;"*

*Go to her,* Phoenix heard something call from inside her own mind, felt her heart hammering, and moved to take a step toward the figure.

*"Blight Chrysanthemum blooms beneath the galaxy, in spring it will grow, and tomorrow it will flower;"*

Phoenix lifted her foot, arms beginning to swing forward — and in the next instant felt a pull at her wrist.

*"My love blooms beneath the galaxy, today I sing of romance, and tomorrow I say goodbye…"*

Phoenix turned to look — but before she could see what had taken hold of her, she was dragged into darkness.

## 3

Darkness.

It pressed into every corner of the classroom like some creature from another world, thick enough to make breathing feel viscous. Phoenix's face was buried in the crook of her arm, her skin prickling from the static generated by her uniform against the desk, as if the entire space were vibrating at some low, inaudible frequency.

"Class—"

Woven into the history teacher's lecture was a voice — flat, mechanical, utterly without emotion.

"E6, 88, 91…"

It didn't sound like it was coming from the school's PA system. It sounded like it was being tapped directly against the inside of her skull. Each syllable carried a faint electronic crackle, like an old telegraph transmitting a signal. Phoenix's temples throbbed. Something ancient and instinctive stirred at the base of her spine.

"Please turn to page two hundred and twenty—"

"E5, 9C, A8…"

The numbers from the broadcast kept coming, but the sound of pages turning that should have filled the classroom was completely absent. Phoenix raised her head slightly, eyelids heavy as lead. She squinted and looked around—

Empty.

Thirty-six desks arranged in perfect rows, their surfaces reflecting a faint cold light, like a row of coffins waiting to be filled.

"Today we'll be covering—"

"E7, AD, 89…"

Phoenix's fingers tightened unconsciously around the edge of the desk, her nails scraping a faint sound from the plastic surface. The broadcast's numerical codes continued — no pattern to them, yet they made her think, inexplicably, of some kind of encryption.

"— the War of Blight."

Five words, and they stung her eardrums. The teacher's voice was like a staple gun, driving each syllable deep into her brain. The unexpected phantom pain hit Phoenix like a jolt — her spine seized as if struck by electricity, and she lurched upright from her seat, her consciousness wrenched out of its drowsy depths.

"The War of Blight was a devastating conflict. Forty-two Roc-class enemy warships led hundreds upon thousands of Void Army vessels in an advance toward the edge of the galaxy…"

The history teacher's voice was unnervingly steady. Phoenix stared at the lectern, trying to make out the teacher's face—

But there was only a blurred outline.

"E4, BD, A0…"

The broadcast numbers continued. Phoenix's throat tightened. She tried, tentatively:

"Teacher… where did everyone go?"

No response.

The history teacher's voice continued as if her question had never existed:

"— We must remember the sacrifice of these four warriors, and work to build our Alliance…"

Phoenix's face had gone pale. The surface of the desk beneath her fingertips felt strangely smooth — wrong — and she pulled her arm away on instinct.

Then the static from the broadcast surged suddenly louder, a piercing noise like countless needles driving into her eardrums—

"88, B0, E6…"

At the same moment, the window curtains flew violently inward. A fierce wind tore through the room. Loose strands of hair whipped across her face. Phoenix spun toward the window. Behind the thrashing curtains, something glowed — faint, wrong, unmistakable.

She hesitated for a moment, then carefully rose from her seat, doing her best to keep the teacher's unsettling blurred outline out of her field of vision. She crossed to the window, pressed down the last of her fear, and raised her hand to pull the curtain aside.

Outside the window was not the school grounds.

It was an endless galaxy.

And suspended within that galaxy were tens of thousands of Void Army warships.

Their silhouettes were like ancient predators, dark-red energy patterns flowing across their hulls like pulsing veins. In the front ranks, hundreds of flagship vessels — the massive warships known as Rocs — were slowly rotating their Doomsday Laser cannons, training them on this classroom.

"88, 91."

The broadcast numbers and the history teacher's voice cut off at the exact same moment. Phoenix's retinas flooded with blinding red light. Her body locked in place, her mind racing in pure terror—

*Is this an illusion? Or is this real?*

*Those numbers…*

Her consciousness collapsed like a breaking dam. Every emotion became a flood. Before the Doomsday Lasers could fire, it tore her apart from the inside.

In the final second before the Doomsday Lasers fired, Phoenix realized something — faster than the fear itself—

*This classroom never existed.*

## 4

The machine let out a sharp alarm.

The mechanical arm rose high, tools retracting into a hidden sterilization compartment — the surgery was over.

The door opened again. A stream of people filed in. First came the familiar faces: Proxymar, Nimbus, Hime, and Zhetass. Behind them, several human Alliance Army officers. Last through the door was a tall, slender man with long violet hair — the current Marshal of the Galactic Alliance Army: Fujin.

When the machine above Phoenix's head released a blaze of sky-blue light, Proxymar exhaled.

"It seems we have a new young warrior."

He looked on with quiet warmth as the visor-shaped device slowly lifted away from the surgical table. He wasn't reading the results displayed on the machine — he was watching the girl who was slowly coming back to herself.

"Proxymar… sir?"

Phoenix's mind was still hazy. It was Fujin who spoke first, and then the room filled with applause and a wave of congratulations:

"Miss Phoenix, congratulations."

"Miss Phoenix, your wish has come true."

"Ha — I only met you once, but I had a feeling about you from the start!"

"Welcome to our ranks, Miss Phoenix."

"Congratulations — this old machine has finally found us another one of our own!"

Phoenix, freshly pulled from the depths of her subconscious, stared at all of it in a daze — and then noticed that her clothes had been changed into a white uniform.

She looked down at her chest. A faint green glow pulsed softly from beneath the fabric, like a small furnace of life.

She studied the unfamiliar light with curiosity, then reached out and poked it twice with her index finger. It tingled slightly — but it was warm.

"This is…"

"This is your core. Miss Phoenix — you are a Shipsian." Marshal Fujin delivered the news she had dreamed of her entire life, his voice gentle.

"I'm… a Shipsian."

Hime helped her sit up, and Phoenix settled cross-legged on the table. She looked herself over — the only two things that had changed were her clothes and this small glowing core.

"Well? Do you feel like a whole new person?" Nimbus adjusted his orange goggles.

Phoenix cleared her mind, closed her eyes, and breathed deeply, listening to whatever was happening inside her.

In all her countless daydreams, this moment had come with wild cheering and leaping for joy. But now that years of dreaming had become real, the lake of her heart barely rippled. News that should have changed the course of her life hadn't made her feel particularly moved at all.

"…Honestly? I feel strangely calm." Phoenix answered. "I thought I'd jump up and shout. But I don't feel that kind of excitement. It's as if…"

"As if?"

"As if somewhere deep down, I already knew this moment was coming. So being here now — it doesn't surprise me."

A silence fell over the room. Everyone looked at her with quiet, warm eyes.

"Actually — where's Disar?"

Phoenix suddenly realized her best friend was nowhere in the crowd. She lifted her head and scanned the faces around her, searching urgently through the gaps between people — but the familiar figure was nowhere to be found. Only the cold light reflected off the metal walls cast uneasy shadows in her eyes.

"Miss Disar—"

Hime's gaze shifted deliberately to the floor. Her voice was still gentle — only now it carried a faint tremor.

"— she is not a Shipsian."

"…?"

*What?*

Phoenix went still.

Disar. Not a Shipsian.

"Could the test have made a mistake?"

Her heart had begun to beat in a way that felt almost pathological — as if it were frantically pumping blood to her brain.

"Disar always scores higher than me on exams. Her physical stats are better too. If I can be a Shipsian, there's no way she isn't—"

"— Miss Phoenix. Genetics don't lie. And the test doesn't make mistakes."

Fujin's voice was gentle, but carried a thread of guilt.

"Two retests. Both blood samples showed the same result. Your closest friend, Miss Disar, does not carry the specific mutation gene that Shipsians develop by age thirteen."

The warmth that had just barely begun to spread through Phoenix's fingertips plunged into ice in an instant. She heard something shatter somewhere deep inside her — the glass-painted scroll she and Disar had been building together since the day they met, now breaking into countless sharp edges, slicing at the walls of her heart, tearing at everything she had believed in.

She thought of the two of them at the spaceport before departure, leaning against each other, imagining what kind of warriors they'd become, adjusting their poses to look cooler.

She thought of all those track days at school sports meets, the two of them always pulling ahead of every other competitor by a wide margin, teachers praising their natural talent and saying they'd make outstanding warriors.

The promise they'd made — to join the Alliance Army together — played back in vivid detail, and every image was now colored with the hue of farewell. Too much to hold. It all jammed in her throat, unspeakable, leaving nothing on her tongue but bitterness.

"Then… where is Disar…"

Phoenix asked, her voice rough.

"Miss Disar woke up two hours before you. Under Galactic Alliance Army Base Visitor Regulation Article 42, non-Shipsian civilians with no military business must depart within 12 hours of arrival." Zhetass tapped a few times on the holographic projector on his wrist, pulling up a surveillance feed. "In fifteen minutes, she'll board the ship the base has arranged for her and return to Ceres Major. She should be at the base port right now — the same place you arrived."

On the surveillance feed, Disar stood alone in the busy port, her heavy bag on her back, watching the platform where the transport ship would soon descend. Quiet. Still.

"No — I can't — I haven't said goodbye yet!"

Phoenix clenched her trembling fists, braced her wrists against the table, and launched herself off the edge. Her still-numb legs nearly gave out beneath her. Hime's arm shot out in time, wrapping around her.

Phoenix wanted to say thank you — but she caught Hime's eyes, and Hime gave a small nod. Phoenix understood. She shot toward the exit like an arrow released from a bow. Several human officers moved to stop her, but Proxymar stepped in and held them all back.

He watched Phoenix's figure disappear, and then said quietly:

"At a crossroads, they need to say goodbye."

## 5

Faster. Faster.

The oversized white uniform restricted the length of her stride, but nothing could slow the frequency of her steps. The sound of canvas soles striking metal floor echoed through the corridor like a countdown, urging her heartbeat forward.
A few tall staff members opened an office door and nearly walked straight into Phoenix. They shouted after her retreating figure, and Phoenix moved as if she could not hear a word of it.

"Hey, watch where you're going!"

"Who let a kid onto the base?"

"Wait, that green light is..."

*Does not matter. Have to make it.*

Phoenix left the corridor noise behind her. She reached the elevator and jabbed at the down button with fingers too stiff to feel anything. She watched the floor indicator drop from 13 to 10, stopping at every level, and in the next second turned toward the stairwell instead. The emergency lighting stretched her shadow long down the stairs, breaking at each corner and reassembling in the light of the next floor.

Three hundred meters? Four hundred?

Not enough. Still too far.

Phoenix had no idea how long she had been running, but she felt no fatigue at all. Her legs were as strong as the moment she started, and could have gone faster still. The ache and numbness she had expected never came. In their place was a warm current, faint but steady, growing outward from the green light at her chest like a vine, winding gently around her legs and feeding them endless strength.

*Up ahead...*

*Turn left.*

She had only walked this route once, yet it was perfectly clear in her mind. The memory of arriving was playing in reverse, forming a navigation that guided every turn.

When the transport vehicles grew more frequent around her, when she finally burst through the massive steel gate at the port entrance, she knew she was almost there.

She caught a glimpse of the giant clock mounted on the wall. The minute hand told her she had eight minutes left.

But in this sprawling port, there were over a hundred berths where a ship could dock.

Phoenix frantically replayed everything she had seen on the holographic screen. The surveillance feed — the letter in the bottom-right corner had been a C.

She looked up and scanned the overhead signs. The large display directly above her read: "A".

There was still time.

The automated transport vehicles crisscrossing the floor blocked her line of sight, but she was absolutely certain Disar was in the other direction.

"Disar — Disar — !!"

She ran again, calling her best friend's name at the top of her lungs. Air rushed into her mouth faster than she could manage it, drying her tongue, forcing the words out in something closer to a shout.

"Disar — where are you —"

"Time check: 11:00."

The automated time announcement swallowed every other sound — her own voice, the rumble of treads on the floor, the roar of ships landing and lifting off. Phoenix thought she heard the cold, chaotic school broadcast from her dream, and the image of that classroom without Disar flashed through her mind. A pain she could not name — the pain of losing someone, of being separated — surged up without warning.

Please. Disar.

"Phoenix — !"

Phoenix froze mid-stride, her weight shifting back, barely managing to stop herself.

"Phoenix, I'm over here —"

"Disar!"

Through the moving machinery and the crowd, Phoenix caught a glimpse of a familiar figure. Backpack on her shoulders, wearing a tea-colored coat with an Alliance Army insignia, standing there waving — it was Disar.

Found her. There was still time.

Phoenix walked quickly toward her, noticing a transport robot spraying disinfectant mist on Disar's shoes.

Disar was smiling, still speaking in her usual bright tone to the breathless Phoenix beside her, as if the collapse of a dream had not touched her at all.

"Oh, Phoenix, you're awake!" Disar blinked. "They said you'd be out for a while longer."

The disinfectant robot raised its nozzle and began spraying Disar's backpack. The mist drifted between them, forming a curtain that vanished almost instantly. Phoenix noticed a small pin on Disar's chest — something that looked like a base souvenir.

"This isn't right..." Phoenix stumbled forward two steps, put her hands on Disar's shoulders, and looked her directly in the eyes. A transport vehicle beeped as it swerved around them. "Something must have gone wrong somewhere..."

"Machines don't lie." Disar smiled — a real smile this time. "Besides, it's a postnatal genetic mutation. That takes luck, doesn't it? Whether it happens or not is pure probability."

Phoenix noticed then that Disar's blue eyes were catching the port lights in a particular way, the same shade as the tips of her own sky-blue hair that had lifted during her run.

"At least let me come back to Ceres Major with you once..." Phoenix's voice began to shake.

"Afraid not." Disar held out her arm for the robot to spray. "I asked one of the staff who brought me here. They said Shipsians who haven't completed their training program can't leave the Prometheus Array."

Phoenix fell silent. She looked away, her hands sliding off Disar's shoulders.

Disar suddenly pointed at the screen overhead. "Oh — look, my ship is coming in."

Phoenix looked up. A dark ship appeared on the screen, glinting with crystal-blue light, beginning its slow descent.

"Disar." Phoenix looked at her profile. "Do you... hate me?"

"Hm? Of course not — we're friends. Why would I hate you?"

"We promised we'd join the Alliance Army together, but now..."

Disar laughed again, and unpinned the small badge from her slightly oversized coat, holding it up for Phoenix to see.

"See this? It's a credential from the Alliance officers. There's a unique ID engraved on it." Disar's voice carried a hint of pride. "It's proof of service. If I ever apply to an Alliance-affiliated academy, or enlist, this pin gets me priority admission. With my grades, getting into the Alliance Army is no challenge — don't you believe in me?"

Phoenix did not know whether to say something self-reproaching or something like a blessing.

"And I already told you — whether someone becomes a Shipsian is pure probability! You have absolutely nothing to feel guilty about!" Disar gave Phoenix's shoulder a firm pat. "You train in combat. I'll study theory. When we enlist at eighteen, we'll meet again in the Alliance Army — and who knows, maybe I'll even outrank you by then!"

Phoenix knew Disar too well. She could hear exactly how much effort was hidden inside those words.

No matter how much sunlight filters through layered clouds, rain still falls. No matter how careful the disguise, something always seeps through — in the angle of the eyes, in how often fingers smooth the fabric of a coat. She could see that Disar's knees were pressed deliberately together, that her left hand was gripping the hem of her coat, that the ends of each sentence were pitched just a little too bright — Phoenix could even see the faint impression of clenched back teeth pressing against her cheek.
"Though... even if I can still become a warrior, knowing you'll never be able to fight like those heroes — it's still a little..."

The PA system suddenly blared a ship-arrival announcement, drowning out the rest of her sentence.

"Disar..."

"Phoenix, I'm heading back! Send me very long emails — even if you're on the Prometheus Array and I'm on Ceres Major —"

Disar jumped up and waved, the hem of her coat sweeping an arc through the air. Her lashes trembled in the spreading sound wave, and something wet evaporated quickly from the corners of her eyes. Beside them, a small vehicle extended a mechanical arm and clipped a warning line to the hooks on both sides of the boarding bridge with a sharp click, separating the two of them.

She heard the ship touch down, felt the impact travel through the floor. A thousand promises wanted to be said.

Every vow crystallized in her throat. Only the simplest one made it out.

"See you when we're grown!"

The words hit the steel walls of the port and came back as echoes.

"Phoenix — goodbye —"

"Disar — goodbye —"

Goodbye is such a light thing, Phoenix thought. Light enough to slip through the gap of a closing hatch. Light enough to be vaporized in an instant by the blue flame of a thruster. Light enough to cling quietly to the fabric of each other's clothes and keep watch from there.

Phoenix watched the point of light disappear from the surveillance screen, and suddenly understood that the stars were guiding two specks of dust into different directions at the same crossroads. On the spiral arm of the galaxy, each of them had begun a journey without the other.

From this moment, the galaxy began its reburn.
